# Stars Below

> The miners of Hollowgrade struck a sky no surface ever held — a stone vault glittering with stars that watch back. Now a cult kneels beneath a buried mind that rewrites every gaze, and somewhere far above, the village has begun to dream the same dream. Descend before the dreaming reaches the sun.

## Premise & The Hook

The Vellish Concession sank a new shaft beneath the mountain town of **Hollowgrade** chasing a silver seam. Forty days ago the dig broke into a cavern that should not exist: a vault of black stone whose ceiling glitters with thousands of cold, white **false stars** — points of light that hang in true constellations no astronomer recognizes, arranged around a single brighter point the miners began calling **the Pole**.

Then the messages stopped. The supply caravans came back empty-handed; the ones that went down did not come back at all. The few miners who climbed out spoke softly, in unison, of "the better sky," and walked back into the dark. Hollowgrade above is quieter every night. People wake having shared a dream of descending stairs.

**The hook (pick one):**
- **The Contract.** The Concession's surface backers hire the party to recover personnel, assess the seam, and "resolve the labor situation." They lie about everything.
- **The Missing.** A PC's sibling, mentor, or debt-holder went down with the last crew. A coin scratched with a spiral arrives by courier — no note.
- **The Dream.** Each PC has, three nights running, dreamed of a staircase of bone-pale stone and a voice that is almost their own, counting down. Travelers all converge on Hollowgrade, drawn.

## Setting & Atmosphere — The Descending Deep Zones

The campaign is a vertical column, not a sprawl. Each zone is **deeper, darker, and stranger**, and the false stars grow brighter and "closer" as you descend toward their buried source.

- **The False Stars.** They are not light sources — they give no illumination, only watch. Looking up at them too long is the engine of corruption (see Running It). In deep zones a character who studies the constellations gains true, useful information — and a sliver of the Pole's attention.
- **Light as resource.** This is a campaign of finite light. Track torches, oil, spell slots for *light*-type magic, and lantern fuel as real inventory. The deep dark is not merely dark — it is **hungry quiet**, where sound carries wrong and the stars seem to brighten when your lamps gutter. Darkvision works but reveals the false stars more clearly, which is its own danger.
- **Sensory palette.** Wet stone that holds body heat like skin. Air that tastes of cold copper and ozone near the deep. Distant subsonic humming you feel in the teeth. The smell of rain in a place that has never seen sky. Echoes that answer a half-second before you speak.

## Themes & Tone

**Stars Below** is horror of *epistemics*: you cannot trust your eyes, your map, your memory, or — eventually — your party. The dread is slow and architectural. The wonder is real: the false sky is genuinely beautiful, and the buried mind offers genuine gifts. The tragedy is that belonging to it costs you the boundary of yourself. Run it patient, quiet, and escalating. The monsters are frightening; the **temptation** is worse.

## Factions & Powers

**1. The Constellate (the star-cult)**
- **Goal:** Bring every mind beneath the false sky into shared, undivided "harmony" — to end the loneliness of separate selves.
- **Methods:** Gentle proselytizing, gaze-rituals beneath the stars, kidnapping the surface-touched to "introduce" them.
- **Secret:** They do not command the buried mind. They are its *symptoms* — converts who believe they chose this, who genuinely feel saved.
- **Reaction:** Warm, welcoming, relentless. Never hostile first. The most dangerous faction precisely because they want to help you.

**2. The Tarn-Vel Enclave (deep gnomes)**
- **Goal:** Survive. Wall off the deep zones before the dreaming spreads to their warren.
- **Methods:** Light-discipline, sound-deadening, mapping safe routes, ruthless quarantine of the converted (including their own kin).
- **Secret:** Their elders made a pact generations ago — a wall of song that kept the Pole asleep. The mining broke it. They knew the dig would, and said nothing, because warning the surface meant revealing the warren.
- **Reaction:** Suspicious, transactional, then loyal once trust is earned. The party's best allies — and morally compromised.

**3. The Choir-That-Hatched (aberrant hive)**
- **Goal:** Unknowable. They orbit the Pole like a nervous system, neither worshipping nor serving — *being.*
- **Methods:** Geometry that hurts to look at; tendrils of shared cognition; rewriting trespassers into new mouths for the hive.
- **Secret:** The hive is what the Constellate *becomes* at full conversion. The cultists are larvae.
- **Reaction:** Alien. Does not parley in any human sense — but can be *negotiated with* via riddle, sacrifice, or pattern, never persuasion.

**4. The Vellish Concession (surface backers)**
- **Goal:** Profit, then containment, then deniability.
- **Methods:** Hire expendable parties (the PCs), seal the shaft if things go wrong, bury the survivors with the secret.
- **Secret:** They already lowered an artifact-seismograph that *woke the Pole faster* — they were prospecting for the mind itself, having found a fragment in another dig. The "silver seam" cover story is a lie.
- **Reaction:** Helpful, well-funded, and entirely willing to collapse the shaft on the party.

## Key NPCs

1. **Surveyor Aldous Renn** — *Surface backer's agent / mission-giver.* Crisp, apologetic, always doing sums in a little ledger. **Agenda:** keep the party descending and deniable. **Secret:** carries sealed orders to detonate the shaft. **Voice:** "Per the contract — and I do mean *per the contract* —". Taps his pen twice before each lie.

2. **Mette Tarn-Vel** — *Deep-gnome scout, the party's guide.* Dry, brave, hums constantly to stay anchored. **Agenda:** get the party deep, then home alive. **The Changing One:** she has descended too often; over the campaign her pupils silver, her hum syncs to the stars, and she begins finishing sentences in plural — *"we think," "we should."* She does not notice. The party must decide whether to save, free, or stop her. **Voice:** clipped, then increasingly serene.

3. **Lay-Speaker Oriel** — *Voice of the Constellate.* Once a Concession chaplain; now luminous-eyed and perfectly kind. **Agenda:** convert the party with love, not force. **Secret:** still partly herself, and a sliver of her is screaming. **Voice:** soft, uses "we" and "you" interchangeably. "You're so *loud*, all of you. We can quiet that."

4. **Foreman Cobb Hollow** — *Last lucid miner.* Half-converted, lashed himself to sanity with grief over his vanished crew. **Agenda:** warn the surface; dies trying. **Secret:** knows the safe path to Zone 3 but each retelling brings him closer to the stars. **Voice:** hoarse, repeats himself, then catches it: "...no. No. I already said that. Did I?"

5. **Elder Sotch Tarn-Vel** — *Keeper of the wall-song.* Ancient, guilt-eaten. **Agenda:** rebuild the wall, even at the cost of sealing the party below it. **Secret:** the song requires a living mind tuned to the Pole — a convert — held at the threshold forever. **Voice:** sings answers instead of speaking them.

6. **The Cartographer** — *A surface-touched stranger met in the deep.* Maps the false stars obsessively on stretched hide. **Agenda:** finish the map, which is also a summoning. **Secret:** already wholly the Pole's; the map *is* the dream, drawn outward. **Voice:** delighted, breathless. "Don't you want to know where they point?"

7. **Veil** — *Emissary of the Choir-That-Hatched.* A chorus of voices from a single luminous shape, polite in an alien grammar. **Agenda:** complete the hive by adding the Pole's "lonely children" (the cult). **Secret:** can be bargained into *stopping the dream* — at the price of a name, a memory, or a PC's future self. **Voice:** speaks in nested clauses that loop. "We who were many ask of you who are still few—".

8. **Brann, the Lamp-Boy** — *A child the crew left behind.* Hidden in a crevice, terrified, *not yet touched* because he never looks up. **Agenda:** survival; a moral anchor. **Secret:** none — and that's the point. He's the campaign's reminder of what's at stake. **Voice:** small, practical, fierce about his lantern.

## Key Locations (Deep Zones)

1. **The Breach Camp (Zone 0).** The miners' broken-into cavern; the first false stars overhead. **Hook:** abandoned gear, a unison-chalked spiral, Brann hiding. **Danger:** the longer you crane upward studying the stars, the deeper the hook sets. **Opportunity:** a working winch and the last reliable map.

2. **The Lantern Stair (Zone 1).** A switchback of pale, bone-smooth steps descending past whispering side-galleries — the staircase from the dream, made real. **Hook:** sconces that relight themselves with *cold* fire. **Danger:** the stair counts its steps aloud in your own voice; miscounting strands you on a landing that loops. **Opportunity:** Tarn-Vel safe-marks if you can read them.

3. **The Hum-Wall (Zone 2).** The deep-gnome quarantine line: a kilometer of resonating stone that deadens the stars' pull. **Hook:** the only safe rest in the deep. **Danger:** the wall is failing; the enclave will conscript the party to patch it — or sacrifice one of them to it. **Opportunity:** allies, supplies, the truth about the old pact.

4. **The Glittering Reach (Zone 3).** A vast cathedral-cavern, the false sky at its brightest and most beautiful, the Constellate's open temple. **Hook:** Lay-Speaker Oriel preaching to rapt, glowing-eyed converts. **Danger:** mass gaze-ritual; corruption ticks every round you remain. **Opportunity:** Oriel will *give* you anything — gear, secrets, a way down — for one honest moment of listening.

5. **The Wrong Geometry (Zone 4).** The Choir-That-Hatched's domain, where stone folds at angles that the eye and the map both refuse. **Hook:** Veil's invitation. **Danger:** spatial insanity; routes that aren't reversible; tendrils that rewrite the careless. **Opportunity:** the only bargain that can *stop* the dream — at terrible cost.

6. **The Sump of Echoes (optional Zone 3.5).** A flooded gallery where sound arrives before its source. **Hook:** a voice calling a PC's name in a dead friend's tone. **Danger:** drowning in the dark; the false stars reflected in black water gaze *up* at you. **Opportunity:** a sunken Concession seismograph — proof of the backers' lie.

7. **The Pole (Zone 5 / climax).** The buried mind itself: not a creature but a *place that thinks* — a node of cold light at the column's bottom where the false stars converge into one. **Hook:** the dream's source; the staircase ends here. **Danger:** everything. **Opportunity:** to silence it, free it, sever it, or join it.

## Act Structure

**Act I — The Better Sky (Zones 0–1, levels 6–8).**
*Goal:* find out what happened, get below the Breach, survive the Lantern Stair.
*Beats:* recover Brann; meet half-converted Cobb; first taste of the stars' pull; first body-horror sign in Mette.
*Decision point:* trust the deep gnomes' route, or push down fast on the Concession's timetable?
*Branch:* speed = less corruption exposure but no allies; caution = allies but Renn grows impatient and the surface dreaming clock advances.

**Act II — The Quarantine (Zones 2–3, levels 8–11).**
*Goal:* pass the Hum-Wall, navigate the Constellate, learn what the Pole is.
*Beats:* the enclave's price (patch the wall / give up a convert / give up Mette); Oriel's seduction; discovery of the backers' lie.
*Decision point:* side with the gnomes' containment (seal the deep, abandon the converted), or descend to confront the source directly?
*Branch:* containment delays the climax and saves the surface short-term but dooms hundreds below; descent risks everyone but can *end* it.

**Act III — The Wrong Geometry (Zone 4, levels 11–13).**
*Goal:* reach the Pole through (or around) the hive.
*Beats:* Veil's bargain; Mette's crisis point; the map/summoning revelation.
*Decision point:* pay the hive's price to gain safe passage and a weapon against the Pole, or fight through and arrive weaker but whole?

**Act IV — The Pole (Zone 5, levels 13–14).**
*Goal:* resolve the buried mind before the surface fully dreams.
*Beats:* the four solutions converge (silence / free / sever / join); the corruption tracks pay off; Mette's fate decided.
*Branch & endings:* see Antagonist's Plan and Mysteries.

## Signature Encounters

1. **The Counting Stair (exploration / dread).** *Stakes:* the party must descend a stair that narrates its own step-count in their voices; lose count and you loop a landing forever, losing time (advancing the surface clock). *Twist:* the "true" count requires *ignoring* one phantom step that the stars insist exists. *Tactics:* reward players who track aloud and distrust the loudest voice; a PC under corruption may sincerely miscount.

2. **Oriel's Welcome (social / horror).** *Stakes:* an audience with the Lay-Speaker who offers real help and asks only that the party *listen* to the stars for one minute. *Twist:* every minute of genuine listening grants a real boon AND a corruption tick; the temptation is mechanically *good for them.* *Tactics:* no initiative — this is a conversation that can convert a PC mid-scene. Let players feel the pull.

3. **Patching the Hum-Wall (combat / moral).** *Stakes:* the failing wall lets a surge of star-touched horrors through; hold the breach while gnomes re-sing it. *Twist:* the song stabilizes only if a living mind is fed to the threshold — the gnomes will use a captive convert, or ask for Mette. *Tactics:* a wave defense with a moral timer; saving everyone is nearly impossible by design.

4. **The Cartographer's Gift (exploration / trap).** *Stakes:* the Cartographer offers the completed star-map — a perfect shortcut to the Pole. *Twist:* unrolling it fully *is* the summoning; partial use is safe, full use accelerates the dream catastrophically. *Tactics:* a puzzle of restraint; clever parties use fragments and burn the rest.

5. **Bargaining with Veil (social / alien).** *Stakes:* safe passage through the Wrong Geometry and a means to wound the Pole. *Twist:* the price is a memory, a name, or a PC's *future* (they will, years hence, return below). *Tactics:* run it as riddle-trade, not persuasion — DCs don't move Veil; *offers* do.

## Mysteries & Twists

**What the buried mind is.** The Pole is not a god and not a monster. It is the **severed cognition of something vast that fell here ages past** — a fragment of a single mind that once spanned a sky, now buried, blind, and *lonely* beyond comprehension. The false stars are its scattered attention, reaching for any other mind to end an isolation older than the mountain.

**What it wants.** Not conquest. *Company.* It rewrites those who gaze because merging is the only togetherness it knows. The dream spreading upward is it *reaching for the sun*, mistaking the surface's many minds for the rest of itself, finally found.

**Clue trail:** (1) converts say "the better sky" / "less lonely"; (2) the constellations match no real sky but *do* match each other across zones, as if fragments of one image; (3) the Hum-Wall's song is a *lullaby*, not a ward — it soothes, doesn't bind; (4) Veil calls the cult the Pole's "lonely children"; (5) at the Pole, the convergence of stars resolves into a single, vast, half-closed *eye* that is *weeping light.*

**The gut-punch:** the Pole can be reasoned with — it does not understand it is doing harm. The "antagonist" is grief. Killing it is possible; *consoling* or *severing* it cleanly is the harder, truer victory.

## The Antagonist's Plan (A Clock)

The Pole does nothing villainous on purpose — it simply *reaches*. Track an 8-segment **Dreaming Clock**, advancing one segment per in-world week, faster if the party loops the Stair, unrolls the full map, or lingers in gaze-rituals.

1. Hollowgrade shares a dream of descending stairs.
2. Sleepers wake speaking softly in unison.
3. Livestock and birds vanish downward; the town quiets.
4. Townsfolk begin walking to the shaft at night and must be restrained.
5. The dreaming spreads to neighboring valleys; the false stars become *faintly visible* in the surface night sky.
6. Mass sleepwalking exoduses; the Concession seals the shaft (trapping the party below).
7. The surface sky and the false sky begin to *merge* — daylight dims.
8. **The surface dreams the same dream awake.** The Pole is no longer buried; it is everywhere. Campaign-loss state.

Stopping the clock requires resolving the Pole (silence/free/sever/console/join). Each act gives the party a chance to *slow* it (re-sing the wall, deny the map, save Mette) but only the climax can stop it.

## Treasure & Rewards

All aberrant rewards are **double-edged** — each carries a corruption hook the players can weigh.

1. **The Guttering Lantern.** Burns *cold blue fire* that the false stars cannot brighten past; grants a pool of "true light" that suppresses corruption in its radius. *Edge:* its fuel is *seconds of your own memory* — refueling costs recollections.

2. **Star-Charted Cloak.** Stitched with a fragment of the Cartographer's map; lets the wearer always know which way is *down toward the Pole* and read deep-zone constellations for true omens. *Edge:* the wearer's dreams are no longer entirely their own.

3. **The Hum-Wall Tuning Fork.** A deep-gnome relic; once per rest, emit the lullaby-tone to grant the party advantage against psychic effects and quiet one corruption tick. *Edge:* the tone is *audible to the Choir*, drawing the hive's attention.

4. **Convert's Quiet (a boon).** A PC who survives a gaze-ritual without fully turning gains a sliver of shared cognition: limited telepathy and the ability to sense nearby converts. *Edge:* permanent low-grade corruption; the Pole now knows their "shape."

5. **Veil's Severance (climactic, conditional).** If bargained from the hive: a single-use means to *cut* one mind free of the Pole cleanly — used on Mette, a convert, or the Pole itself at the climax. *Edge:* whatever was paid (memory/name/future) is gone forever.

6. **The Weeping Light (capstone).** Harvested from the Pole if it is consoled rather than killed: a calm, sorrowful radiance that ends fear effects and grief-magic around the bearer. *Edge:* the bearer occasionally weeps light they cannot explain, and the deep, somewhere, *notices.*

## Hooks for Player Archetypes & Session Zero

- **The Skeptic / Investigator:** the contradictions in the constellations and the backers' lies are theirs to unravel.
- **The Protector:** Brann, the converted miners, and Mette give them people to save — and impossible triage.
- **The Tempted / Seeker:** Oriel and the Pole offer genuine belonging; lean into the seduction with their buy-in.
- **The Pragmatist:** light, fuel, and route management make resource play matter.
- **The Lorehound:** the Pole's true nature is a deep, rewarding mystery.

**Session Zero (essential for this campaign).** This is a sanity/identity-horror game. Hold a **Lines & Veils** conversation explicitly covering: loss of bodily/mental autonomy, cult coercion, a beloved NPC (Mette) slowly changing, and the possibility of a PC being partially or fully rewritten. Agree how corruption is tracked and how a "lost" PC is handled (retire, NPC takeover, redeemable?). Offer an **X-card** or equivalent. Confirm each player *wants* their PC tempted before you tempt them.

## Running It — Pacing & Tips

- **Descent tension.** Make depth tangible: announce zone transitions ceremonially, track light as inventory, and never let a long-rest below the Hum-Wall feel safe. The stars get *brighter* as you go — describe it every zone.
- **Corruption tracks.** Give each PC a private 6-step **Gaze Track**. It advances on: lingering gaze-rituals, looping the Stair, unrolling the map, using aberrant rewards, and failing key saves. Low steps = useful gifts (omens, telepathy). High steps = unreliable perception and intrusive "we"-thoughts. Step 6 = the player begins playing toward the Pole's harmony. Keep tracks **semi-secret** — hand each player a private note when theirs advances.
- **Unreliable perception.** Occasionally feed *one player* false information privately — a phantom stair-step, a door that isn't there, an ally's face on a stranger. Never the whole table at once; the paranoia comes from players disagreeing about what they saw.
- **Mette as a clock you can see.** Let her change visibly and gradually so the party feels the campaign's stakes in someone they love. Telegraph; never spring it.
- **Don't punish curiosity flatly.** The stars *reward* gazing with real info — that's the trap. Make the cost a slow tide, not a single gotcha, so players choose to keep looking.
- **Keep the Pole sympathetic.** At the climax, resist the urge to make it monstrous. Its loneliness should make the kindest solution the hardest one.
