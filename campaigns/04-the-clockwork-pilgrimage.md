# The Clockwork Pilgrimage

> Once a generation the bridges of impossible brass align, and the Pilgrim leaves the platform between worlds — a train so long it threads through realities, bound for the Engine at the heart of creation. You bought a ticket. Now the cars are uncoupling into the void, and something aboard wants the pilgrimage to fail forever.

## Premise & The Hook

The **Pilgrim** is a train the size of a pilgrimage road. It runs the **Brass Meridian** — a route of bridges that only exists when the planes drift into rare alignment, an event called the **Conjunct**, which lasts roughly forty days. At the far end waits the **Engine of First Motion**, the mechanism whose turning is said to *cause* time. To ride to the Engine and back is the holiest journey most mortals will ever attempt; tickets are inherited, won, or stolen, and a seat is a story you tell for the rest of your life.

The party boards at the **Embarkation Platform of Quel** with whatever reason brought them: a debt to repay at the Engine, a question only First Motion can answer, a fugitive flight, or simple pilgrimage. Within the first day, **Car 47 uncouples** mid-bridge and falls silently into the gulf between worlds — passengers and all. The Conductors call it an accident. It is not. The route is breaking, the schedule is slipping, and the party is about to learn that the Pilgrim has made this run thousands of times and has *never once* lost a car. Until now.

**The hook that sticks:** the only people aboard with no fixed duty, no faction loyalty, and freedom to walk the whole train — are the party.

## Setting & Atmosphere — The Pilgrim & the Brass Meridian

The Pilgrim is a **living dungeon and a moving town**. Over a hundred cars, each its own neighborhood: a dining car that is a covered market, a chapel car where bells ring at every border-crossing, a garden car under a glass roof full of borrowed sky, a boiler-cathedral where furnace-priests feed coal that is not coal. Couplings groan like ship timbers. The corridor windows show *outside* — and outside is whatever world the bridge currently spans.

**Sensory anchors:** the smell of hot brass and incense; the floor's eternal sway you stop noticing by day three; the **chime** that sounds before every crossing, after which the windows go to mirror-black for a held breath, then open on a new sky. Clocks aboard disagree with each other on purpose. Some cars run a day in an hour; the **Long Car** takes a week to walk and you arrive having aged none. Distance is negotiable. Time is luggage.

The route crosses **bridges of impossible brass** strung between drifting worlds — each "station" is a world the Pilgrim halts beside long enough to take on water, fuel, passengers, and trouble.

## Themes & Tone

Lead with **wonder, then unease.** Every car should first delight, then reveal a crack. The campaign asks: *Is the destination worth the road? Do we owe the stranger in the next seat?* The sabotage is real and rising, but the heart is the **community of the train** — a hundred small lives the party can save or fail. Keep menace ambient and the marvels generous. Awe is the default register; horror is a spice.

## Factions & Powers

**The Conductors' Compact** — the train's clergy-crew in brass-buttoned greatcoats.
*Goal:* deliver every soul to the Engine and home again, as they have for ten generations. *Methods:* ritual maintenance, rigid schedule, the **Coupling Rite** that binds cars to the spine. *Secret:* they know the route is failing and have known for two Conjuncts; they hid it to prevent panic. *Reaction:* defensive, then desperate; will recruit the party once denial collapses.

**The Unmade Choir** — a stowaway cult that worships the void between worlds, the **Hush**.
*Goal:* return all things to the silence before First Motion. *Methods:* sabotage the couplings, sing cars loose, recruit the grieving. *Secret:* their prophet rode a previous Pilgrim and *came back wrong* — touched by the Hush. *Reaction:* patient, evangelical, never where you look.

**The Heralds of the Engine** — clockwork envoys sent *up the line* from First Motion itself.
*Goal:* protect the Engine; the route's failure is a wound it can feel. *Methods:* precise, lawful, alien courtesy; they speak in scheduled tenses. *Secret:* the Engine is *dying*, and the Heralds were sent to find a replacement steward — possibly a mortal. *Reaction:* helpful but inhuman; they value the schedule over any single life.

**The Vermillion Wager** — rival pilgrims: a chartered company of treasure-hunters and debt-collectors riding first class.
*Goal:* reach the Engine first and bargain a fortune from First Motion. *Methods:* bribery, leverage, a private rail-yacht car. *Secret:* their patron sold a soul to *board* and the bill comes due at the Engine. *Reaction:* opportunistic allies, faithless rivals.

## Key NPCs

**Conductor-Prime Ossian Vale** — head of the Compact. Weary nobility; speaks in railway idiom ("we keep to the timetable, even through grief"). *Secret:* he ordered the last Conjunct's failing data destroyed and the guilt is unmaking him. *Mannerism:* winds a stopped pocket-watch he never looks at.

**Junior Conductor Pell** — eager, brass-polished, the party's first friend aboard. *Secret:* her brother was on Car 47. *Voice:* over-explains everything, then catches herself. The campaign's emotional barometer.

**The Hushbinder** — masked prophet of the Unmade Choir, voice like wind under a door. *Agenda:* uncouple the Pilgrim car by car until nothing remains to reach the Engine. *Secret:* once **Pilgrim Aurel Saine**, beloved navigator, lost to the Hush a generation ago and grieving still. *Mannerism:* never finishes a sentence; lets silence land it.

**Herald Tertius "Third-Bell"** — clockwork envoy, all etiquette and ticking. *Agenda:* assess whether any aboard could steward the Engine. *Secret:* Third-Bell is *running down* and will stop before the journey ends. *Voice:* refers to events in the future perfect ("you will have helped me").

**Madame Calliope Brrecord** — proprietor of the dining-market car, fixer, gossip-broker. *Agenda:* keep her car prosperous and herself indispensable. *Secret:* she's smuggled the Choir's coal-that-isn't aboard for years, not knowing what it was. *Mannerism:* feeds you while she interrogates you.

**Captain Reyes Vermillion** — head of the rival Wager. Charming, transactional, secretly terrified. *Agenda:* outrun his patron's debt. *Secret:* his ticket is a forgery; he is not, technically, a pilgrim, and the Engine will know. *Voice:* toasts to things that haven't happened.

**Old Tare** — ancient porter who has ridden every Pilgrim for two hundred years (time aboard is strange). *Agenda:* none but the train's survival. *Secret:* he remembers the *first* uncoupling, in a Conjunct the Compact erased from record. *Voice:* speaks in route-rumor and riddle; the party's lore wellspring.

**The Child in Car Nine** — a passenger who never ages and won't say their name. *Secret:* a fragment of the Engine's consciousness, riding incognito, learning what mortals are worth saving. *Mannerism:* asks devastating questions, then offers candy.

## Key Locations

**The Coupling Spine (interstitial)** — the brass joints between every car, hummed taut by the Coupling Rite. *Hook:* the crime scene of every sabotage. *Danger:* working here mid-bridge means the void *right there*; a fumble is a fall forever. *Opportunity:* clue-rich; the Choir's tampering leaves a residue of *too-perfect silence*.

**The Dining-Market Car** — Calliope's domain, a covered bazaar that smells of forty worlds. *Hook:* social hub, rumor exchange, every faction eats here. *Danger:* the smuggled coal is stored beneath the floor. *Opportunity:* the party's home base and information market.

**Station: Bellumbra, the City That Rings** — a world of cathedral-bells where sound is currency and silence is illegal. *Hook:* take on the choir-fuel that powers the chime. *Danger:* the Unmade Choir's homeland sympathies run deep here. *Opportunity:* recruit a bell-priest who can *hear* the sabotage coming.

**The Long Car** — a single coach that takes a week to walk, full of doors to memories. *Hook:* shortcut through time; reach the engine-cars "early." *Danger:* people lose decades; one door opens on a passenger's worst day on loop. *Opportunity:* confront an NPC's hidden past directly.

**Station: The Drowned Orrery** — a world that is a sunken model of the cosmos, planets the size of moons rolling through black water. *Hook:* the bridge here is half-collapsed; the party must reset a planetary gear to extend the rail. *Danger:* pressure, leviathan custodians, a Choir ambush. *Opportunity:* recover a navigation-relic that predicts the next failing coupling.

**The Boiler-Cathedral** — the furnace-priests' nave, where the train's not-fire burns. *Hook:* the engine's health is read in the flames. *Danger:* feed it the wrong fuel and a car ahead lurches loose. *Opportunity:* the throughline clock is *visible* here — players can literally watch the route unravel.

**Station: Terminus Verge — The Engine of First Motion** — the destination, a mechanism the size of a sky, every gear a law of reality. *Hook:* the climax. *Danger:* the Engine is dying and the Hush is at the gates. *Opportunity:* the final choice (see Twists).

## Act Structure

**Act I — All Aboard (Levels 5–7).** *Goal:* establish wonder, board, survive the first uncoupling. *Beats:* embarkation; meet the Compact and the Wager; Car 47 falls; the party is deputized to investigate. *Decision Point:* sound the alarm and spark panic, or work quietly with Vale? *Branch:* public investigation builds passenger allies but alerts the Choir; quiet work keeps an edge but isolates the party.

**Act II — The Failing Line (Levels 8–11).** *Goal:* trace the sabotage across three to four station-worlds while cars keep slipping. *Beats:* episodic station adventures (Bellumbra, Drowned Orrery, the Long Car) each yield one clue toward the Hushbinder *and* one toward the route's deeper sickness. *Decision Point:* the party learns the Compact hid prior failures — do they expose Vale or shield him to keep the crew functional? *Branch:* exposure fractures the crew but frees Pell and Tare to help; shielding keeps order at a moral cost.

**Act III — The Hush Rising (Levels 12–14).** *Goal:* unmask the Hushbinder and confront the truth that the Engine itself is dying. *Beats:* the Choir's open move; a major car (or station) lost or saved by the party; the Hushbinder revealed as Aurel Saine; Third-Bell's confession about a needed steward. *Decision Point:* save the route, or hear the Choir's argument that endings are mercy? *Branch:* sets the stakes and allies for the finale.

**Act IV — Terminus Verge (Levels 14–15).** *Goal:* reach the dying Engine, resolve the sabotage, choose the fate of First Motion. *Beats:* the final bridge; the Hushbinder's last stand; the Engine's offer of stewardship. *Decision Point (the campaign's heart):* repair the Engine and pay its price; let a PC or NPC become its new steward; or accept the Choir's ending and remake the route entirely.

## Signature Encounters

**The Uncoupling (Act I).** A car begins to slip mid-bridge while passengers are still inside. *Stakes:* lives, ticking. *Twist:* the coupling wasn't cut — it was *sung* loose; a Choir agent is aboard the slipping car. *Tactics:* skill-challenge under fire; reaching the joint means crossing the exterior over the void.

**The Bell-Market of Bellumbra (Act II).** A social heist for choir-fuel where silence is a capital crime and the party must barter in *sound*. *Stakes:* refuel or strand the train. *Twist:* the seller is Choir; the "fuel" carries a Hush-seed. *Tactics:* reputation, performance, and noticing the catch before it's loaded aboard.

**Resetting the Drowned Orrery (Act II).** Underwater exploration to realign a planetary gear and rebuild a broken bridge, racing a Choir strike team. *Stakes:* the rail's continuation. *Twist:* the leviathan custodian isn't hostile — it's *grieving* a lost world and can be reasoned with. *Tactics:* environmental puzzle + ambush; rewards the navigation-relic.

**The Long Car Confession (Act II/III).** A walk through a coach of living memories where a chosen NPC's secret stands behind a door. *Stakes:* trust and lore. *Twist:* one door is the party's *own* future on this train — a glimpse of the finale's cost. *Tactics:* roleplay-forward; combat optional and tragic.

**The Hushbinder Unmasked (Act III/IV).** Confrontation as the Choir tries to uncouple the engine-cars from the train entirely. *Stakes:* the whole pilgrimage. *Twist:* the Hushbinder *can be talked down* — Aurel Saine still grieves the world they lost, and a PC who learned that lore can reach them. *Tactics:* phased fight that can become a negotiation; the Hush itself bleeds into the car as a hazard.

## Mysteries & Twists

**Who is uncoupling the train?** Surface answer: the Unmade Choir, via sabotage. **True answer:** the Choir is only *accelerating* a collapse already underway — **the Engine of First Motion is dying**, and a dying Engine cannot hold the route taut. The Hushbinder isn't causing the end; they're *welcoming* it.

**Clue trail:** (1) the slipped couplings show no cut, only *unnatural silence* — sabotage by sound. (2) Old Tare remembers an erased prior uncoupling — the failures predate this Choir. (3) The Boiler-Cathedral flames burn *colder* each crossing — the Engine weakens. (4) Third-Bell was sent to find a *steward*, which only makes sense if the current one is failing. (5) The Child in Car Nine knows things no passenger could — a fragment of the Engine, sizing up mortals. (6) The Hushbinder is Aurel Saine, lost a generation ago — the void doesn't only take, it *keeps*.

**The hidden truth:** the pilgrimage was never only about reaching the Engine. It was the Engine reaching for *pilgrims* — searching, generation by generation, for someone worthy to inherit the turning of time.

## The Antagonist's Plan — A Clock (if the party does nothing)

1. **The First Slip** — a car uncouples; the Compact calls it accident, buys silence.
2. **The Quiet Spread** — Choir agents seed Hush along the spine; chimes begin to falter.
3. **A Station Stranded** — refueling fails at a world; the train limps, schedule cracking.
4. **The Crew Breaks** — Vale's cover-up surfaces; the Compact fractures; rituals lapse.
5. **The Engine-Cars Threatened** — the Choir moves on the spine before First Motion; the holy cars begin to drift.
6. **The Route Unravels** — bridges collapse behind and ahead; the Pilgrim is stranded between worlds.
7. **The Hush Arrives** — the Engine stops, the Conjunct closes, and the Pilgrim — train, town, and all aboard — falls silent into the void forever.

Each ignored clue or lost station advances the clock one tick. Decisive party action can rewind it.

## Treasure & Rewards

**The Conductor's Crossing-Whistle** — sound it to safely traverse one impossible bridge or close one Hush-breach per Conjunct; calms a slipping coupling for a round.

**Aetherglass Compass (the Orrery relic)** — points not north but toward the *next failing coupling*; once per day, reveals the safest path across any planar gulf.

**Ticket of the Long Car** — step through into a chosen remembered place and return having spent no outside time; one use, then it yellows to a normal stub.

**Hush-Sealed Lantern** — burns a flame of captured silence; in darkness it grants steady nerve against fear and reveals Choir taint, but tempts its bearer toward stillness.

**Brass Pilgrim's Token** — proof of journey; the Engine and its Heralds honor the bearer. Mechanically: a once-per-rest reroll framed as "the schedule favors you."

**Coupling-Rite Sigil (boon)** — a PC trained by the Compact can bind two things — objects, allies, even concepts — as if coupled, sharing fate briefly. Story-flexible, GM-arbitrated.

## Hooks for Player Archetypes & Session Zero

- **The Seeker** carries a question only First Motion can answer — let the Engine eventually *answer it.*
- **The Faithful** treats the pilgrimage as devotion; the Choir's argument will test them.
- **The Fugitive** boarded to escape; the train is the one place a debt can't follow — until the Wager arrives.
- **The Tinker** is enraptured by the machinery; give them the spine, the boiler, the relics.
- **The Local** has a tie to a station-world the route will cross — make it a homecoming or a reckoning.

**Session Zero prompts:** *Why is your seat worth more than gold? What do you carry that you'd give the Engine to keep? Who did you leave on the platform?* Establish each PC's stake in *arriving*, so the threat of never arriving lands personally.

## Running It — Pacing & Tips

**Wonder first, plot second.** Open every car with a marvel the players can *touch* before any danger shows. Spend description budget lavishly on crossings — the chime, the mirror-black windows, the new sky.

**Keep the throughline visible.** The Boiler-Cathedral flame and the slipping couplings are your clock made physical; show them dimming so episodic stations never feel disconnected. End most sessions on a chime or a small slip.

**Make the train a town.** Name porters, vendors, children. Recurring faces make later losses hurt and later saves feel earned. Let players adopt an NPC.

**Episodic, but cumulative.** Each station is self-contained for one-shot flexibility, but always yields one mystery clue and one route-clock consequence. Drop or reorder stations freely.

**Lean on time-weirdness as a feature.** Use the Long Car and disagreeing clocks to fast-forward, flash back, or buy the party "early" arrivals — and to make the planar travel feel genuinely strange rather than merely scenic.

**The finale is a choice, not a fight.** Foreshadow the stewardship offer from Act II so the ending lands as a decision the players have been quietly preparing for all along.
