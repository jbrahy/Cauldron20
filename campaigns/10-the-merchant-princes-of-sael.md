# The Merchant Princes of Sael

> *In Sael, a knife is just a contract that skipped the paperwork. The houses smile across the canals, the gondolas carry secrets instead of silk, and every favor you do today is a leash someone tightens tomorrow. You came here with nothing but nerve — climb high enough and the city is yours. Climb too slow and it belongs to someone you never met.*

## Premise & The Hook

Sael is a republic in name and an auction in practice. Eleven **Merchant Houses** sit the Argent Council; their feuds are fought with contracts, marriages, poisons, and deniable freelancers. The party are those freelancers — a **crew** of fixers, smugglers, and spies who arrived broke and unaffiliated, which in Sael is the only honest position left.

**The Hook:** A dying gondolier named Pell14 presses a wax-sealed cylinder into the party's hands and dies in their boat with a crossbow bolt in his back. Inside: a **bearer-bond** worth 500 crowns, redeemable only at House Veltrecci's countinghouse — and a second, smaller scroll listing eleven names, eight of them already crossed out. Whoever cashes that bond becomes interesting to everyone in Sael at once. Welcome to the ladder.

The bond is real. The list is the thread that, pulled, unravels a conspiracy that has spent a decade quietly *buying Sael itself* — house by house, vote by vote — and is now three purchases from owning the Council outright.

---

## Setting & Atmosphere

**Sael** is a city built across a drowned river-delta, its districts threaded by green-black canals and stitched together by arched footbridges slick with moss. There are no carts; there are **gondolas, barges, and stilted walkways**. Fog rolls in by the third bell and turns lantern-light into floating coins. The city smells of brine, candle-tallow, citrus from the import quays, and the sweet rot of canal water.

Wealth is worn openly and aggressively — house colors dyed into sleeves, **counting-glass** monocles, lacquered debt-tokens jangling at belts like jewelry. The poor live *above* the rich here, in the leaning upper tenements where the fog never quite clears, looking down on the marble water-palaces below.

The wards:
- **The Goldwaters** — house palaces, the Argent Council, white stone and private guard.
- **The Quill Reach** — markets, banks, notaries; deals signed and broken by the hour.
- **Tidewrack** — docks, smugglers, the fish-and-flesh markets, where the party likely sleeps.
- **The Spindles** — vertical slums on rotten pilings, home of the gangs and the desperate.
- **The Drowned Quarter** — flooded ruins of old Sael, half-submerged, half-forgotten, fully dangerous.

## Themes & Tone

Witty and dangerous, like a velvet glove with a stiletto sewn into the seam. Banter at parties; blood in the canals an hour later. The campaign is **about ambition and its price**: every rung up the ladder costs a piece of who you were. Favors are warmer currency than gold and far more binding. The throughline question is **"who owns a city?"** — and the horrifying answer the conspiracy offers is *"whoever simply buys it while everyone else is busy stabbing each other."* Keep it player-driven: the GM presents jobs and pressures, never a rail.

## Factions & Powers

### House Veltrecci — "The Bank Behind the Throne"
- **Goal:** Total financial control of the Council via debt held over rival houses.
- **Methods:** Loans, bearer-bonds, ruinous interest, plausible blackmail.
- **Secret:** Veltrecci is **already insolvent** — propped up by a single mysterious foreign creditor (the conspiracy). They're the first domino, not the spider.
- **Reaction to party:** Sees the crew as deniable hands. Generous patron, treacherous master.

### House Ondemar — "The Spice Admirals"
- **Goal:** Monopolize the southern trade routes; humiliate Veltrecci.
- **Methods:** Privateers, dock control, "accidents" at sea.
- **Secret:** Their fleet is smuggling *quill* (a memory-numbing narcotic) under the spice manifests. Matriarch Surahn loathes it but can't survive without the margin.
- **Reaction:** Rough, transactional, loyal if you bleed for them.

### The Notary's Guild ("The Inkhands")
- **Goal:** Be indispensable — every contract, will, and vote passes through them.
- **Methods:** Information brokerage, forgery, the legal stranglehold.
- **Secret:** A faction within the Guild is **forging house deeds for the conspiracy**, legally transferring ownership before anyone notices.
- **Reaction:** Neutral, mercenary, will sell the party out and to.

### The Tideless ("The Drowned Court")
- **Goal:** The underworld — smuggling, protection, the *quill* trade, votes bought in the Spindles.
- **Methods:** Violence, fear, genuine community loyalty in the slums.
- **Secret:** Their boss, **Mother Cresse**, is the one ally who's figured out the conspiracy — because *they tried to buy her too*. She wants the party to be her informants.
- **Reaction:** Suspicious, then fiercely protective once trust is earned.

### The Ashen Ledger (the conspiracy — hidden until Act II)
- **Goal:** Own all eleven houses through proxies and forged deeds, then dissolve the Council and rule by contract.
- **Methods:** Foreign capital, the Inkhand forgers, assassination of holdouts (the **crossed-out list**).
- **Secret:** It's run by **Lord-Auditor Veil**, a discreet "financial consultant" everyone has met and no one remembers hiring.
- **Reaction:** Will recruit the party with absurd generosity — then erase them.

## Key NPCs

1. **Donna Liesl Veltrecci** — *Patron.* Razor-elegant banker, all warmth and no mercy. Twirls a debt-token while she talks. *Secret:* knows her house is failing, gambling everything on the party. *Voice:* "Money is just a story we agree to believe. Let me tell you yours."
2. **Admiral Surahn Ondemar** — *Patron.* Salt-scarred, blunt, drinks like a sailor. *Secret:* ashamed of the quill trade, looking for an exit. *Mannerism:* taps a brass spyglass on the table when lying.
3. **Mother Cresse** — *Patron/ally.* The Tideless boss; looks like a kindly fishwife, isn't. *Secret:* the only one tracking the conspiracy. *Voice:* soft, grandmotherly, terrifying. "Eat first. We'll discuss who dies after."
4. **Magister Quill-Wright Avo** — *Rival/broker.* Head of the Inkhands. Immaculate, pedantic. *Secret:* doesn't know his own juniors are forging for the Ledger. *Mannerism:* corrects everyone's grammar mid-threat.
5. **Lord-Auditor Veil** — *Hidden antagonist.* Pleasant, forgettable, beautifully dressed. *Secret:* runs the Ashen Ledger. *Voice:* "I'm not here to take anything. I'm here to *settle accounts*." People forget his face within the hour — a clue in itself.
6. **Rook** — *Rival crew leader.* Charming gutter-prince who runs the rival freelancers, the **Cinderboys**. *Secret:* desperate, in debt to the Ledger, will betray anyone to clear it. *Mannerism:* flips a coin he never lets land.
7. **Sable In-Vesh** — *Fixer/contact.* Information broker in Tidewrack who first hires the party. *Secret:* triple agent, sells everyone's secrets including yours. *Voice:* fast, jittery, addicted to quill.
8. **Brother Pell** — *Wildcard.* A canal-priest of the drowned god who tends the dead in the canals. *Secret:* fishes corpses for the Ledger and keeps a ledger of his own. *Mannerism:* speaks to the bodies he recovers.

## Key Locations

1. **The Argent Countinghouse (Quill Reach)** — *Hook:* where the bond is cashed. *Danger/Opportunity:* vaults full of leverage; cashing the bond puts the party on every radar.
2. **The Sunken Bell (Tidewrack)** — *Hook:* the crew's local tavern and job board. *Opportunity:* Sable holds court here; rumors, contacts, the start of every night.
3. **Veltrecci Water-Palace (Goldwaters)** — *Hook:* glittering masquerades where deals are sealed. *Danger:* every guest is armed with a secret and half with a blade.
4. **The Drowned Court (Drowned Quarter)** — *Hook:* Mother Cresse's flooded throne-ruin. *Danger:* tide-flooded, gang-held; *Opportunity:* the truest ally in Sael.
5. **The Inkhall (Quill Reach)** — *Hook:* the Notary Guild's archive of every deed in Sael. *Opportunity:* the forged deeds are filed *here* — proof of the conspiracy, behind impossible security.
6. **The Spindles' Highwalk** — *Hook:* slum bridges where votes and quill change hands. *Danger:* Rook's Cinderboys hunt here; a fall is sixty feet into black water.
7. **The Auditor's Office (location unknown until Act III)** — *Hook:* a quiet rented room nobody remembers renting. *Danger:* the heart of the Ledger; entering it means the war is on.

## Act Structure

### Act I — "Small Fish" (Levels 1–2)
- **Goal:** Survive cashing the bond; build a reputation; pick first patrons.
- **Beats:** Pello's death → the bond → first jobs for Veltrecci/Ondemar/Tideless → meet rival crew Rook.
- **Decision Point:** Whose colors do you wear first? Each patron offers a competing first job *on the same night*.
- **Branch:** Reputation begins splitting along house lines. The crossed-out list nags.

### Act II — "Made" (Levels 3–4)
- **Goal:** Become a name. Discover the conspiracy exists.
- **Beats:** A holdout on the list is murdered while under the party's protection → a forged deed surfaces → Mother Cresse reveals the Ashen Ledger → Veltrecci's insolvency leaks.
- **Decision Point:** Expose the Ledger now (and be disbelieved/hunted) or keep climbing and gather proof?
- **Branch:** Align with a house to fight the Ledger, or play the Ledger to climb faster?

### Act III — "The Auction" (Levels 5–6)
- **Goal:** Stop (or join) the takeover before the final three deeds transfer.
- **Beats:** The Inkhall heist for the forged deeds → Rook's betrayal → Veil makes the party an offer → a house falls publicly.
- **Decision Point:** Whose side? Houses, Tideless, the Ledger, or your own crew making a play for a Council seat.
- **Branch:** The endgame faction war is shaped entirely by accumulated favors.

### Act IV — "Princes" (Level 7)
- **Goal:** The final vote / final blade. Decide who owns Sael.
- **Beats:** Confront Veil in the Auditor's Office; the Council vote; the crew's own ambitions cash out.
- **Resolution:** The party may save the republic, seize a house, become the new power, or burn it all into the canals.

## Signature Encounters

1. **The Double-Booked Job** *(social/heist, Act I)* — Two patrons hire the crew to steal the *same* ledger from the same party on the same night. **Stakes:** first real reputation. **Twist:** the ledger proves Veltrecci's insolvency. **Tactics:** crowd, guards, a rival team (Rook) also reaching for it.
2. **The Protected Man** *(escort/intrigue, Act II)* — Guard a holdout merchant overnight. **Stakes:** a life and the list. **Twist:** the assassin is invisible-to-memory (a Ledger agent); he's killed *despite* the party doing everything right — proving an enemy they can't yet see. **Tactics:** locked townhouse, false servants, the kill happens off a failed Perception, not combat.
3. **The Masquerade Vote** *(social, Act II/III)* — Sway a Council vote at the Veltrecci masquerade. **Stakes:** which house gains power. **Twist:** half the "nobles" are Ledger proxies wearing borrowed faces. **Tactics:** social conflict clock; quill-laced wine; a knife in the gardens.
4. **The Inkhall Heist** *(heist, Act III)* — Steal the forged deeds from the deepest Guild archive. **Stakes:** the only hard proof. **Twist:** the deeds are *legally perfect* — stealing them is itself a crime that brands the party. **Tactics:** wards keyed to house-blood, a flooding lower vault, a time-locked door, Magister Avo arriving early.
5. **The Auditor's Offer** *(social/combat, Act IV)* — Veil invites the crew to *join* and name their price. **Stakes:** everything. **Twist:** accepting is genuinely the easiest path to power — and the campaign supports taking it. **Tactics:** no fight unless they refuse; then the most-favored allies arrive based on banked reputation.

## Mysteries & Twists

The hidden truth: a foreign syndicate, the **Ashen Ledger**, has spent a decade *legally buying Sael* through forged-then-validated deeds, proxy nobles, and Veltrecci's debt. Lord-Auditor Veil is its face — and is somehow **unmemorable by design** (an enchantment, a curse, or simply terrifying tradecraft — GM's choice).

**Clue trail:**
1. The crossed-out list — holdouts being eliminated.
2. A deed in the Inkhall with *yesterday's* ink but a *ten-year-old* date.
3. Veltrecci's single foreign creditor with no name on file.
4. Three "different" nobles who all bank through the same shell, the **Greyfont Trust**.
5. Witnesses who can't describe the man who paid them — *the memory gap is the fingerprint.*
6. The final deed transfer dated to the next Council session — the clock's zero hour.

**Twist options:** Mother Cresse was offered partnership and refused (reliable ally). Rook already took the deal (the betrayer). One PC patron is secretly a proxy without knowing it.

---

## The Antagonist's Plan (a clock)

**The Ashen Ledger's Takeover — an 8-segment clock.** Advance one segment per in-world week the party ignores it, or per major Ledger victory.

1. Veltrecci's debt called — house bankrupt, seat purchasable.
2. Two minor houses' deeds forged and filed.
3. A holdout assassinated; a third house falls.
4. Greyfont Trust gains a Council vote-bloc.
5. Quill floods the Spindles, buying the slum vote.
6. The Inkhall seals its archives "for audit" (proof locked away).
7. Final three deeds transfer; majority secured.
8. **Council dissolved by contract. Sael belongs to the Ledger.** Veil rules from a room no one remembers.

If the party never acts, Sael is quietly, legally conquered without a single public battle.

## Treasure & Rewards

1. **Property:** *The Sunken Bell* — the crew can buy/win their tavern HQ, granting a safe house, a rumor-stream, and rentable rooms.
2. **The Favor-Bank:** Banked house *favors* (see Running It) — spendable to call in guards, open doors, erase a debt, or buy a vote.
3. **Contacts:** Mother Cresse's network (free canal-travel + intel), Sable's broker-line, a tame Inkhand forger.
4. **Unique item — *The Counting-Glass of Veltrecci*:** a monocle that reveals the true monetary value and ownership of anything looked through it (perfect for spotting forged deeds).
5. **Unique item — *Pello's Bond-Cylinder*:** holds one bearer-bond that *always* cashes, once per arc, for a scaling sum — but every cashing pings the Ledger.
6. **A Council Seat:** the ultimate reward — a defeated/vacated house's chair, making the crew a *Merchant Prince* in truth.

## Hooks for Player Archetypes & Session Zero

- **The Smuggler:** owes Ondemar a debt; knows the dock-routes.
- **The Spy:** has a secret patron pulling strings (great Ledger-proxy candidate).
- **The Fixer/Face:** the crew's social engine; starts with one banked favor.
- **The Muscle:** ex–house guard, blackballed, with a grudge to settle.
- **The Climber:** wants a Council seat above all — feed them ambition.

**Session Zero — Build the Crew:** As a group, define: (a) the crew's **name and colors**, (b) how they met (a shared first job gone sideways), (c) their **HQ aspiration**, (d) one shared enemy and one shared debt. Each PC names one NPC they already know from the roster. The crew starts with **2 banked favors** and a reputation of "Nobodies."

## Running It — Pacing & Tips

**The city is the dungeon.** Don't draw a path; present pressures (patrons, the clock, rivals) and let the crew choose targets. Every session: offer **3 jobs** from different factions so choice always has cost.

**The Favor/Reputation Economy:** Track each house's opinion on a simple **−3…+3 scale**. Jobs shift it. Crossing a +2 unlocks a *Favor* token (a spendable plot intervention). Crossing −2 makes that house a soft enemy. **Reputation tiers:** Nobodies → Known → Made → Princes — gate jobs and respect by tier.

**Faction Clocks:** Run the Ledger's 8-clock plus a small clock per house's scheme. Advance them *between* sessions so the world moves without the party. Show the ticks: a posted bankruptcy notice, a body in a canal.

**Improvising Jobs:** Roll the tables below. A job = **target + employer + complication**. Let outcomes feed reputation, never a binary win/lose. Reward clever talk and quiet theft as much as combat — most Sael "fights" should be avoidable and most expensive when they aren't.

**Tone-keeping:** Open scenes with one sensory detail and one piece of gossip. End sessions on an offer, a debt, or a betrayal.
