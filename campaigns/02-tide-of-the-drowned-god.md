# Tide of the Drowned God

> The sea does not rise to drown you. It rises because something below is finally turning over in its sleep — and dreaming your name.

## At a Glance

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Level Range** | 3–12 |
| **Party Size** | 3–5 |
| **Est. Length** | 12–16 sessions across 3 acts (island-hop, then sunken descent) |
| **Tone** | Nautical cosmic horror; dread, creeping wrongness, oceanic awe |
| **Core Themes** | Sleep vs. waking, the price of mercy, the smallness of human will against the deep |
| **Content Advisories** | Drowning, body horror, psychological corruption, cult violence, harm to coastal communities. Calibrate in Session Zero. |
| **Best For** | Tables who like investigation, mood, hard choices, and an ending where "win" is ambiguous |

## Premise & The Hook

The **Saltmarrow Reach** is a chain of nine storm-lashed islands strung across a cold green sea. For three generations it has been quiet, poor, and pious. Then, forty nights ago, every soul on the Reach woke at the same hour from the same dream: a vast pale shape uncoiling in a black trench, an eye the size of a harbor opening just a sliver, and a single bell-deep tone that lives in the back teeth.

Since then: fish wash ashore still alive, mouths working in eerie unison as if **singing**. Tide charts no longer hold. A drowned man walked home to his wife and sat at her table dripping for a day before she screamed. And the **Choir of the Open Eye**, a cult thought extinct, has resurfaced — buying boats, salt, and silence.

The god in the trench is **Oumr-Belu, the Drowned God**, neither dead nor alive but *dreaming*, and its dream is leaking into the world. The Choir wants to wake it and be remade in its image. Others want it kept asleep at any cost. The party arrives in the middle.

**Hooks (pick or combine):**
- **The Contract.** The merchant guild of nearby Carrack's Landing hasn't heard from its Reach factor in six weeks and three ships are overdue. They hire the party to find out why — and recover the cargo if anyone can.
- **The Dream Found You.** One or more PCs began dreaming the Drowned God's dream *before* setting foot on the Reach. They don't know why. (See Session Zero.)
- **The Lighthouse Letter.** A frantic, half-coherent letter arrives from a lighthouse keeper named **Halvre Onn**, addressed to "anyone who still keeps faith with the dry world." It includes a tide chart that is mathematically impossible.

## Setting & Atmosphere

The Reach is grey water under a sky the color of a healing bruise. Islands are black rock and wind-bent pine, ringed with kelp forests and the bones of old wrecks. Villages cling to leeward coves; everyone is related to everyone; everyone is tired in the way of people who haven't slept right in a month.

Lean on **sensory wrongness**, not gore. The bread tastes faintly of brine no matter how it's baked. Gulls have stopped crying. A becalmed sea is *worse* than a storm — it means something below is listening. Reflections in standing water lag a half-second behind. When a town "sings," it isn't music; it's three hundred throats finding the same low note in their sleep, and not waking.

The Reach is run as a **light sea-hexcrawl**: islands are nodes, sea-lanes are edges, and crossing a lane costs time and a roll on the **Ocean Omens** table (Appendix). Storms, fog-banks, and the deep's attention make every voyage an event.

## Themes & Tone

This campaign is about **sleep as mercy** and **waking as violence**. Oumr-Belu is not evil; it is *large*, and it dreams the way a tide pulls — without malice and without stopping. The horror is that no one is the villain enough to deserve what's coming, and the party's best outcome may be to keep a thing asleep rather than slay it.

It's also about **the cost of certainty**. The Choir is sure. The opposing Keepers are sure. Both are willing to drown a town to be right. The party's doubt is the campaign's only mercy.

To land it: make the dreams personal, make the sea patient, and never let a "safe" island stay safe for long. Reward investigation over force. Let players *feel* the corruption as a tradeoff they chose, not a status effect inflicted on them.

## Factions & Powers

**The Choir of the Open Eye (doomsday cult).**
- *Goal:* Wake Oumr-Belu and be "unmade and remade" as its first-born.
- *Methods:* Dream-induction rituals, drowning-baptisms, sabotage of wards, recruiting the despairing.
- *Secret:* Their prophet **Maren Sloe** has already begun to change — she no longer needs to breathe air — and she is terrified, not exalted.
- *Reaction:* Court the party first ("you dreamed too, didn't you?"); kill them only when refused.

**The Keepers of the Sounding Bell (zealous wardens).**
- *Goal:* Keep the god asleep forever, by any means.
- *Methods:* Maintaining the **Sounding Bells** (sonic wards) on each island; ruthless quarantine of "the dream-sick."
- *Secret:* The Keepers caused the original drowning of the temple centuries ago — and the last time the god stirred, they **sank an entire island of dreamers** to silence it. They will do it again.
- *Reaction:* Useful, knowledgeable, and willing to sacrifice the party "for the Reach."

**The Reach Fisherfolk (the drowning commons).**
- *Goal:* Survive; not lose more family to the sea or the cults.
- *Methods:* Old charms, stubbornness, hiding the dream-sick from the Keepers.
- *Secret:* Many quietly half-believe the Choir is right — that waking is better than this endless dread.
- *Reaction:* Suspicious of outsiders, but desperate for someone unaligned. The party's true potential allies.

**The Carrack's Landing Guild (mainland money).**
- *Goal:* Recover assets, reopen trade, keep the dream off the mainland.
- *Methods:* Coin, leverage, deniable agents.
- *Secret:* They already know the dream is spreading to the mainland and are quietly evacuating their own families.
- *Reaction:* Will fund the party as long as it's profitable; will abandon them the instant the Reach is written off.

## Key NPCs

- **Halvre Onn** — Lighthouse keeper, the party's first ally. Insomniac, kind, brilliant, unraveling. Maps the tides by hand. *Secret:* he stopped sleeping because the moment he dreams, he wakes facing the sea, walking toward it. *Voice:* over-explains, laughs at the wrong moments.
- **Maren Sloe** — Prophet of the Choir. Was a midwife; lost three children to a wreck. *Secret:* her transformation is real and irreversible, and she'd undo it if she could. *Voice:* gentle, motherly, devastatingly reasonable. Never raises her voice.
- **Warden Esk Drummel** — Senior Keeper. Iron-spined, exhausted, honest about being a monster. *Secret:* he personally rang the bell that drowned the last dreaming island, and hears the children still. *Voice:* clipped, military, occasionally cracks.
- **Old Nelda Faar** — Fisherwoman, unofficial mayor of the village of Cull. *Secret:* keeps her dream-sick grandson hidden in a sea-cave; she'll do anything to protect him. *Voice:* dry, profane, sees through lies instantly.
- **Brother Tace** — Choir defector hiding among the fisherfolk. *Secret:* he carries the only intact copy of the ritual to *re-sink* the temple — and doesn't know what it costs. *Voice:* stammers, flinches at the sound of bells.
- **The Singing Boy** — A drowned child who walked home. Speaks only in the god's dream-tone, but *answers questions truthfully* if you can bear to listen. *Secret:* he is a living antenna for Oumr-Belu and can be used to gauge how awake it is. *Voice:* a flat, layered chord that resolves into words.
- **Captain Vossa Quill** — Smuggler with the only fast ship willing to sail the deep lanes. *Secret:* she's already taken Choir gold to deliver something to the trench. *Voice:* breezy, mercenary, allergic to commitment.
- **Doctor Sennet Aute** — Mainland physician studying the dream-sickness "objectively." *Secret:* she's infecting herself in stages to map the corruption, and is further gone than she admits. *Voice:* clinical, curious, frighteningly calm.

## Key Locations

- **Onn's Light (the lighthouse).** Hub and refuge. *Hook:* Halvre's impossible tide chart predicts the descent point. *Danger:* the light is failing; in the dark, the sea climbs the stairs.
- **Cull (fishing village).** The "safe" town. *Hook:* the Singing Boy is hidden here; the Choir is recruiting from grief. *Opportunity:* the fisherfolk become allies if defended. *Danger:* one night, the whole village sings.
- **The Bell-Tower of Skarrow.** A Keeper sonic ward, half the islands' protection. *Hook:* the bell is cracking; someone is sabotaging it. *Danger:* if it falls silent, the dream floods the eastern Reach overnight.
- **The Gut (drowned-ship graveyard).** A fog-locked bay of wrecks fused into a single rotting reef. *Hook:* charts and confessions are buried in the holds. *Danger:* the wrecks are inhabited; the drowned crews still keep their posts.
- **Maren's Atoll (Choir stronghold).** A flooded ring-island where the cult performs baptisms. *Hook:* the ritual to wake the god is staged here. *Opportunity:* Maren can be turned — she's the campaign's tragic hinge.
- **The Singing Shoal.** A shallow where the dead fish gather to sing. *Hook:* standing here lets you "hear" how awake the god is (the doomsday clock made audible). *Danger:* lingering pulls a Corruption check.
- **The Temple of the Open Eye (the descent, Act III).** A drowned ziggurat in the trench, air-pocketed and impossibly intact. *Hook:* the climax. *Danger:* everything. Reality, pressure, and time all behave like the inside of a dream.

## Act Structure

**Act I — The Reach Wakes (levels 3–5).** *Goal:* arrive, investigate, learn the shape of the horror. *Beats:* the impossible tide chart; the Singing Boy; first contact with both cults. *Decision Point:* who do you trust — the certain Keepers or the gentle Choir? *Branch:* aiding either faction unlocks different knowledge and closes other doors.

**Act II — Island by Island (levels 6–9).** *Goal:* secure (or sabotage) the Sounding Bells, find the descent point, choose a final intent. *Beats:* a town sings and must be saved or sacrificed; Brother Tace's re-sinking ritual surfaces; Maren's offer. *Decision Point:* **Wake it, kill it, or re-sink it?** *Branch:* each path requires different components gathered across islands.

**Act III — The Descent (levels 10–12).** *Goal:* reach the temple and enact the chosen rite. *Beats:* the voyage to the trench; the dream-logic temple; confronting Oumr-Belu's half-open eye. *Decision Point:* the final rite — and whether to pay its price. *Branch:* multiple endings (below).

## Signature Encounters

1. **The Town That Sings (social/horror).** Cull falls into the chorus at midnight. PCs must wake key people without waking the *wrong* ones (a Choir cell wakes hostile). *Twist:* the only reliable way to break the song is the Sounding Bell — which the Keepers will use to drown the whole village if the party rings it. *Resolution:* improvisation over a fixed solution; reward clever, costly choices.

2. **Boarding the Gut (exploration/combat).** A crawl through fused wrecks for a buried confession. *Twist:* the drowned crews aren't hostile until you take what they guard. *Tactics:* timed, claustrophobic, rising water; treat the environment as the monster.

3. **Maren's Baptism (social/moral).** The party infiltrates or is invited to a Choir baptism. *Twist:* the "victim" volunteered and is at peace. *Resolution:* no clean combat win; this is about whether the party can watch, intervene, or be tempted.

4. **The Bell Falls (combat/defense).** Saboteurs (Choir, or a desperate fisher) crack the Skarrow bell mid-fight. *Twist:* mid-encounter the dream floods in — enemies and allies alike start to sing; initiative gets weird. *Tactics:* a defense scenario with a corruption-clock running.

5. **The Half-Open Eye (climax).** At the temple's heart, the god's eye opens a sliver. Not a stat-block fight — a contest of *will, rite, and sacrifice*. *Twist:* attacking it wakes it faster. *Resolution:* the chosen rite resolves as a skill-and-cost gauntlet; victory is measured in what you're willing to lose.

## Mysteries & Twists

- **The Keepers are the original sin.** They drowned the temple — and an island of innocents — generations ago. *Clues:* mismatched bell inscriptions, Drummel's nightmares, a sealed hold in the Gut.
- **Maren's transformation is a warning, not a promise.** *Clues:* she avoids fresh water, never sleeps, weeps at the Singing Shoal.
- **The dream is already on the mainland.** *Clues:* the Guild's quiet evacuations, Doctor Aute's notes, a Carrack's Landing child who hums the tone.
- **Re-sinking has a price no one mentions.** Tace's ritual requires a living dreamer to take the god's dream *into themselves* and descend with it. *Clues:* a redacted line in the ritual, Tace's flinching, the last island's lone survivor.

## The Antagonist's Plan (a Clock)

Run as an 8-segment clock. Advance ~1 segment per in-world week, faster if the party ignores the Bells or aids the Choir.

1. **Stirring.** Shared dreams, singing fish. (Start.)
2. **The First Walk.** Drowned dead return home.
3. **The Choir Rises.** Recruitment surges; first Bell sabotaged.
4. **A Town Sings.** An island falls into the chorus and doesn't fully wake.
5. **The Eye Cracks.** Tides fail across the Reach; voyages become deadly.
6. **The Shoal Spreads.** Dream-sickness reaches the mainland's edge.
7. **The Atoll Opens.** The Choir completes the waking baptism; the trench glows.
8. **The God Wakes.** Oumr-Belu rises. The Reach sinks. The dream becomes the world.

## Treasure & Rewards

- **The Soundless Bell** — a hand-bell that, when rung, silences the god's tone in a wide radius for one scene. *Tradeoff:* each use deafens the ringer for a day and pulls a Corruption check — the silence is *its* silence.
- **Halvre's Impossible Chart** — lets the party predict tides and the descent point; advantage on sea-navigation. *Story:* it slowly rewrites itself as the god wakes.
- **Brine-Touched Lungs (boon)** — a PC who survives a baptism can breathe water. *Tradeoff:* they can no longer fully rest on dry land; one permanent corruption mark.
- **Maren's Pearl** — a black pearl that shows the bearer true dreams. *Tradeoff:* it shows the god's dream too, and it's listening back.
- **The Re-Sinking Litany** — Tace's ritual; the only "keep it asleep forever" win condition. *Cost:* requires a willing dreamer to descend and stay.
- **Drowned Coin** — Choir payment that always returns to your pocket. *Tradeoff:* spend it and a drowned thing follows it home.

## Hooks for Player Archetypes & Session Zero

- **The Faithful / Cleric:** their god is silent on the Reach — why? A crisis of faith engine.
- **The Scholar / Wizard:** the dream is a *text* to be read; reward translation and theory.
- **The Protector / Fighter or Paladin:** give them a town (Cull) to defend and impossible triage.
- **The Outsider / Rogue or Warlock:** they dreamed the dream first; they may already be marked.
- **The Healer / Druid or Bard:** the corruption is a sickness to be fought — and a temptation to study.

**Session Zero (do not skip):** This is cosmic horror with drowning, bodily change, and harm to communities. Establish lines and veils. Offer an opt-in/opt-out for which PCs "dreamed first." Use a corruption track that players *choose* to advance for power. Agree on a safety tool (X-card or check-in). Promise them up front: there is no clean victory, and that's the point.

## Running It — Pacing & Tips

- **Make the sea a character.** Narrate it before every voyage. Calm water is a threat. Use the Omens table to keep the deep unpredictable.
- **Dreams should lie.** Never let a dream be reliable exposition. Mix one true clue with two false ones; let players argue about which.
- **Dread is rhythm, not volume.** Alternate quiet, human island scenes with one sharp wrongness. Don't escalate every scene — let the safe ones breathe so the horror lands.
- **Corruption is a bargain.** Always frame it as a choice with upside, never a punishment. The horror is that they *want* to take it.
- **Keep the clock visible-ish.** Let players hear the god waking (the Shoal, the Boy) so the doom feels earned, not arbitrary.
- **No clean win is fine.** Coach yourself to honor the three real endings — wake, kill (which still drowns the Reach), or re-sink (which costs a dreamer). Let the table sit in the cost.

## Appendix: Tables

**Table A — Ocean Omens (d8, roll on each sea-lane crossing)**

| d8 | Omen |
|---|---|
| 1 | A becalming. No wind for hours; the crew starts to hum without noticing. |
| 2 | Fish surface and "watch" the ship, mouths working in unison. |
| 3 | A wreck appears that isn't on any chart, lit from within. |
| 4 | The water turns mirror-still and reflections lag a half-second. |
| 5 | A bell tolls underwater, felt in the teeth. (Advance clock pressure.) |
| 6 | A drowned figure pacing the party's wake, never closer, never gone. |
| 7 | Rain that tastes of someone else's tears; brief shared half-dream. |
| 8 | Clear sailing — *too* clear. Everyone arrives having lost an hour. |

**Table B — Corruption Symptoms (d6, when a PC advances the track)**

| d6 | Symptom |
|---|---|
| 1 | Eyes film over grey-green; you see better in dark water. |
| 2 | You hum the god's tone when stressed and don't notice. |
| 3 | Fresh water tastes foul; you crave brine. |
| 4 | Your reflection blinks a beat late. |
| 5 | You wake facing the nearest sea, having walked toward it. |
| 6 | You understand the Singing Boy perfectly — and answer back. |

**Table C — Shared Nightmares (d6, roll on nights the party sleeps near the sea)**

| d6 | Dream |
|---|---|
| 1 | The pale shape uncoils; the eye opens a fraction wider than last time. |
| 2 | You are drowning, calm, and grateful — and you wake gasping. |
| 3 | A loved one, drowned, sets a place for you at a flooded table. |
| 4 | The whole Reach sings and you are the only one still silent. |
| 5 | You walk the temple stairs and almost remember building them. |
| 6 | Oumr-Belu says your name — correctly — and asks you to come down. |
