# The Hollow Crown

> A king's golden age was borrowed against tomorrow — and the lender from the Court of Thorns has come to collect, in seasons, in memory, and in blood-of-the-crown.

## Premise & The Hook

Eleven generations ago, the first Hollow King, **Aldric the Sun-Mantled**, knelt in a frost-rimed grove and struck a bargain with **Lady Maelvaine of the Court of Thorns**. In exchange for two centuries of impossible prosperity — harvests that never failed, peace that never broke, a kingdom called **Vesselgaard** that the world envied — the crown promised a *tithe*. The tithe is paid quietly: a forgotten spring here, a stillborn heir there, a beloved memory that simply ceases to be. For two hundred years the payments were small and the king's accountants were the only ones who knew.

The two centuries are up. The principal is due. And the current monarch, **King Halloran the Mild**, did not know the loan existed until the thornwood began climbing his castle walls.

**Hooks (pick or layer):**
- **The Vanishing Spring.** Vesselgaard has had no spring this year. It is the height of what should be May, and the orchards stand black and bare. The party is hired — by a guild, a noble, or the crown itself — to find out why the season did not come.
- **The Forgotten Princess.** A grieving courtier insists the king had a daughter, **Eirlys**, who no longer exists — not dead, *unmade*. No record, no portrait, no memory but his. He'll pay anything to a party who believes him. (He is right.)
- **A Debt in Your Own Name.** A PC discovers an ancestor signed a minor sub-tithe to the same Court — a family heirloom blooms with black thorns, and a fey envoy arrives to "discuss terms."

## Setting & Atmosphere

Vesselgaard is a temperate river-kingdom that has been *too* perfect for too long: hedgerows trimmed by no hand, bees that never sting, cathedral bells that ring themselves on feast days. The wrongness is in the lushness. Now the bargain is unwinding, and the kingdom is reverting to a wild, hungry fairy-tale logic.

Sensory signatures to lean on at the table:
- **The smell of crushed green** everywhere thornwood grows — sweet, then rotten.
- **Seasons running backward or stalling** — frost on roses, snow that tastes of summer.
- **Sourceless music** — a hurdy-gurdy three rooms away that is never in any room.
- **People who half-remember** — townsfolk who weep without knowing why, toasting a queen no one can name.

The Court of Thorns itself is a realm of perpetual, beautiful late autumn — gold light, falling leaves that never land, a banquet that has been ongoing for a thousand years and will not let a guest leave hungry or leave at all.

## Themes & Tone

This is a story about the bill coming due — about a generation inheriting a comfort it did not earn and a debt it did not sign. Maelvaine is not wrong that she is owed. The party's real work is not "kill the fey" but *renegotiate the terms of a contract no living person consented to.*

To land it at the table: make the prosperity **visible and lovable first** — let the players enjoy Vesselgaard before they understand its cost. Make every payment **personal** — not "the kingdom loses spring," but "the innkeeper who fed you for free can no longer remember her son's face." Refuse the clean win: the best endings are *bittersweet trades*, where someone pays so others don't have to.

## Factions & Powers

**The Crown of Vesselgaard** — *Goal:* survive the collection without the kingdom learning the throne sold them. *Methods:* secrecy, the Sealed Treasury, desperate diplomacy. *Secret:* King Halloran has already offered Maelvaine his own remaining memories as partial payment, and is quietly forgetting his own name. *Reaction:* hires the party, then panics as they learn too much; may turn on them to protect the secret.

**The Court of Thorns** — *Goal:* collect the tithe in full, by the letter of the contract. *Methods:* envoys, glamour, literal-minded bargains, time-bending. *Secret:* the original contract has a flaw — Aldric pledged "the blood of the crown in perpetuity," but the crown line was *broken and reforged* six generations ago; the debt may be voidable. Maelvaine knows. *Reaction:* coolly courteous; respects clever mortals, despises liars and contract-breakers.

**The Order of the Unwithered** — *Goal:* a secret monastic society that has *managed* the tithe for 200 years, choosing who is "spent." *Methods:* records in the Sealed Treasury, ritual, quiet murder of those who'd expose them. *Secret:* they've been skimming — paying the Court extra to keep certain noble families exempt, including their own bloodlines. *Reaction:* see the party as a leak to be plugged; offer help, then betray.

**The Reclaimers** — *Goal:* a populist movement of the "spent" — people who've lost children, memories, springs — who have begun to *remember* as the bargain frays. *Methods:* riots, leaks, vigilante justice. *Secret:* their charismatic leader, **Wren**, is herself a partial fey changeling planted by the Court generations ago, and doesn't know it. *Reaction:* natural allies who can become a mob the party must restrain.

**The Hollow Choir** — *Goal:* the unquiet remnants of everyone ever "unmade" by the tithe, a chorus of erased souls. *Methods:* haunting, whispering forgotten truths, demanding to be remembered. *Secret:* they are the best source of the contract's true history — and they can restore a memory the Court erased, at a price. *Reaction:* not malicious, only desperate to exist again.

## Key NPCs

- **King Halloran the Mild** — gentle, guilt-cracked monarch losing his memories by the week. *Secret:* offered himself as payment. *Hook:* trails off mid-sentence and looks to a servant to finish his own thoughts.
- **Lady Maelvaine of the Court of Thorns** — the lender; ancient, courteous, genuinely sorrowful about what she must do. *Secret:* she loved Aldric, and the tithe was as much heartbreak as commerce. *Hook:* speaks always in the present tense about the dead — "Aldric *is* a stubborn man."
- **Castellan Brannoc Veil** — head of the Order of the Unwithered, pragmatic to the point of monstrous. *Secret:* exempted his own grandchildren. *Hook:* counts on his fingers when he lies.
- **Wren of the Ashmarket** — fierce leader of the Reclaimers, lost a daughter she can't quite remember. *Secret:* she is a changeling; her "daughter" is the original stolen child. *Hook:* hums a lullaby she doesn't know she knows.
- **Eirlys** — the unmade princess, now a half-real girl who flickers at the edge of vision in the castle. *Secret:* she is the living interest on the loan; restoring her costs someone else's existence. *Hook:* her words arrive a half-second after her lips move.
- **Thornwarden Sil** — Maelvaine's envoy, all teeth and protocol. *Secret:* admires mortal courage and would rather lose honorably than win by trickery. *Hook:* offers food at every meeting; refusing or accepting both matter.
- **Old Maren** — the innkeeper at the Greenpenny, who has fed the party for free out of inexplicable love. *Secret:* she is being "spent" in real time, forgetting one person she loves per week. *Hook:* sets an extra place at the table for someone whose name she's lost.
- **Archivist Tamsin Crole** — disgraced royal historian who first noticed the deletions in the records. *Secret:* keeps a forbidden ledger of every name the kingdom has forgotten. *Hook:* compulsively writes names on her own forearm in ink.

## Key Locations

- **The Greenpenny Inn** — warm hearth, free meals, a town that loves the party for no reason. *Hook/Danger:* the affection is glamour-residue from old prosperity; as the bargain fails, the town's love curdles to suspicion.
- **Castle Vesselgaard, the Solar** — Halloran's chambers, slowly overgrown with thornwood that grows toward the throne. *Hook/Opportunity:* the wall-thorns spell out contract terms in their growth-rings if read by candlelight.
- **The Sealed Treasury** — not gold, but the Order's archive of 200 years of payments. *Danger:* trapped, guarded, and the records *re-edit themselves* as new payments come due.
- **The Withered Orchard** — where spring failed; black trees, frost-locked blossoms, the first place Eirlys appears. *Opportunity:* the orchard is a thin place; the Court can be reached here on the equinox.
- **The Ashmarket** — the Reclaimers' stronghold in the city's forgotten quarter, built of grief and contraband. *Danger:* a tinderbox; one wrong word starts a riot.
- **The Thornwood Threshold** — the frost-rimed grove where Aldric first knelt; the doorway to the Court. *Danger:* time runs differently; an hour inside can be a season outside.
- **The Court of Thorns, the Endless Banquet** — Maelvaine's hall. *Danger:* eat the food and you owe the Court; refuse all hospitality and you insult it. Both bind you.

## Act Structure

**Act I — The Stalled Spring (Levels 1–3).** *Goal:* investigate why the season failed. *Beats:* arrive in a too-perfect Vesselgaard; meet Old Maren and the Greenpenny; discover the Withered Orchard; first flicker of Eirlys; a thornwood envoy delivers a "courtesy notice" to the crown. *Decision Point:* take the crown's coin and its secrecy oath, or side with Archivist Crole and dig where you're forbidden. *Branch:* crown route grants access but blinds you to the Order's crimes; Crole route gives truth but makes you fugitives.

**Act II — The Ledger of the Forgotten (Levels 4–6).** *Goal:* uncover what is being paid and who decides. *Beats:* break into the Sealed Treasury; expose the Order's skimming; meet Wren and the Reclaimers; learn Eirlys is "interest"; the Hollow Choir offers a forbidden memory. *Decision Point:* expose the Order publicly (igniting the Reclaimers), or keep the secret to prevent a war. *Branch:* exposure gives you the mob as muscle but pushes the kingdom toward collapse; secrecy keeps order but lets the Order keep killing.

**Act III — Terms of the Court (Levels 7–9).** *Goal:* reach Maelvaine and learn the contract's true shape. *Beats:* cross the Thornwood Threshold; navigate the Endless Banquet's hospitality traps; learn the broken-bloodline flaw; Maelvaine reveals her grief and her offer. *Decision Point:* exploit the contract's flaw to void the debt (and free Vesselgaard but doom the Court, which prosperity also sustains), honor the debt with a chosen sacrifice, or attempt a new bargain. *Branch:* each path reshapes the finale.

**Act IV — The Hollow Crown (Level 10).** *Goal:* settle the debt. *Beats:* the chosen path plays out — a contract-court duel of wits, a battle against Thornwarden Sil's vanguard, or a renegotiation where the party themselves become the new collateral. *Decision Point:* who pays? *Resolution:* there is always a cost — the question is who bears it, and whether the party can make it meaningful.

## Signature Encounters

**The Courtesy Notice (social, Act I).** Thornwarden Sil arrives at court to deliver the collection notice. *Stakes:* first impressions bind future negotiations. *Twist:* Sil offers each PC a small, free gift; accepting creates a minor personal debt logged for later. *Resolution:* graciousness and reading the rules of fey etiquette matter more than any roll.

**The Self-Editing Archive (exploration, Act II).** The Sealed Treasury's records rewrite themselves. *Stakes:* extract the true ledger before it deletes a name the party needs. *Twist:* the room "spends" a random NPC the party cares about as the cost of their intrusion. *Tactics:* time pressure; copying a record requires speaking the forgotten name aloud to anchor it.

**The Ashmarket Powderkeg (social/combat, Act II).** A Reclaimer rally tips toward riot. *Stakes:* the city, and the party's standing with Wren. *Twist:* a planted Order agent throws the first stone to discredit the movement. *Resolution:* de-escalation, a public truth, or letting it burn — each has lasting consequences.

**The Endless Banquet (exploration/social, Act III).** Dinner at the Court of Thorns. *Stakes:* leave the Court free and unindebted. *Twist:* every course is a contract; the salt is a vow, the wine a confession. *Tactics:* trade riddles and favors; the only safe move is to *bring a gift* that outvalues the meal.

**The Settling (climactic, Act IV).** Whatever form the finale takes. *Stakes:* the fate of seasons, memories, and heirs. *Twist:* the contract's broken-bloodline flaw means *no one* legally owes the debt — which means prosperity itself was theft, and someone must volunteer to make it just. *Resolution:* victory is measured in how few are unmade, never in zero.

## Mysteries & Twists

- **The tithe is paid in the forgotten.** *Clues:* Maren's vanishing memories; Crole's ink-stained arm; townsfolk toasting a nameless queen.
- **Eirlys is living interest, not a dead princess.** *Clues:* she flickers but never decays; the courtier who alone remembers her; her delayed voice.
- **The Order skims and exempts its own.** *Clues:* Brannoc's grandchildren are suspiciously untouched; ledger gaps; finger-counting tells.
- **Wren is a changeling, and her "daughter" is the true child.** *Clues:* the lullaby she shouldn't know; her immunity to the tithe; Court records naming her.
- **The contract is voidable — the bloodline broke.** *Clues:* the reforged-crown history six generations back; the thornwood growth-rings; Maelvaine's reluctance to enforce.
- **Maelvaine loved Aldric.** *Clues:* present-tense speech about the dead; the grief beneath her courtesy; a portrait in the Court that matches the first king.

## The Antagonist's Plan (a Clock)

If the party does nothing, the Court collects on schedule:

1. **Spring is repossessed** — the failed season becomes permanent across Vesselgaard.
2. **Memories are called in** — beloved figures (starting with Maren's family) are unmade, city-wide.
3. **The heirs are claimed** — Halloran's line, present and future, ceases; Eirlys is fully retracted.
4. **The crown is hollowed** — Halloran forgets he is king; governance collapses.
5. **The thornwood crowns the castle** — Vesselgaard reverts to a fey-wild, folded into the Court's autumn.
6. **The debt is "paid"** — Vesselgaard becomes a story other kingdoms tell to frighten children, and the Court grows by one more drowned realm.

## Treasure & Rewards

- **The Unwithered Ledger** — Crole's forbidden book; speaking a name within it can restore one unmade person — *once*, at the cost of forgetting someone of equal worth to the speaker.
- **Maelvaine's Courtesy** — a single fey favor, freely given; redeemable for one impossible thing, but the Court always collects courtesy in kind.
- **Crown of Thorns and Gold** — Aldric's circlet; lets the wearer perceive and read the terms of any active bargain, but slowly grows thorns inward.
- **The Half-Real Locket** — Eirlys's keepsake; lets a PC step briefly out of memory (untraceable, unseen) — but each use erodes one of the PC's own memories.
- **Seedheart of the Vanished Spring** — if planted, restores one season to one place forever; there is only one seed.
- **The Choir's Whisper** — a one-use boon from the Hollow Choir; reveals any single hidden truth in the campaign, but adds the user's voice to their chorus for a year.

## Hooks for Player Archetypes & Session Zero

- **The Noble/Royal-blooded PC:** tie their lineage to the original signatories — or to an exempted Order family. Their blood may *be* the debt.
- **The Scholar/Investigator:** Crole becomes a mentor and a moral mirror; reward note-taking.
- **The Fey-touched/Warlock:** their patron and the Court of Thorns share a rivalry; offer alliance or extortion.
- **The Local/Commoner:** root them in the Greenpenny; make Maren's unmaking *personal*.
- **The Cynic/Mercenary:** start them on the crown's payroll, then make the secrecy oath cost them.

**Session Zero notes:** flag the heavy themes (loss of children, memory erasure, dementia-like decline) and confirm consent. Establish lines and veils; the unmaking of loved NPCs is the campaign's emotional engine and should be opt-in. Set the expectation early and clearly: **there is no flawless victory here — only bargains, and the dignity of who chooses to pay.**

## Running It — Pacing & Tips

- **Make them love it first.** Spend real time in a sunny, generous Vesselgaard before the rot shows. Grief needs a foundation.
- **Spend NPCs, not stats.** The tithe should cost *relationships* the players have built. Track who they care about and collect from there.
- **Honor fey logic.** The Court keeps its word to the letter and expects the same. Reward players who read contracts carefully; let literalism cut both ways.
- **Keep Maelvaine sympathetic.** Never let her become a generic villain. Her grief is the campaign's heart.
- **Let choices scar.** Every act's decision point should foreclose something. Don't soften the foreclosures.
- **The clock is your tension.** When the party stalls, advance the antagonist clock one step and show the cost on screen.
- **End on a trade, not a triumph.** Steer the climax toward "who pays and why it matters," and give the payer a moment of grace.
