# Crown of Cinders

> Three hundred years ago the sky bled fire and a dragon crowned herself queen of ash. She was not slain — she was *buried*, and the bell that buried her is cracking. When it falls silent, she wakes. You begin as nobodies in a forgotten valley. You will end at the last bridge with the world's only torch.

## Premise & The Hook

The realm of **Vael Anhar** rings a great bronze bell every dawn from the **Choir Spire** at its heart. Children are told it keeps the weather kind. The truth, buried in a sealed crypt no living priest can open, is that the bell's tone is a binding — it holds **Suvarra the Ember-Crowned**, the dragon-tyrant who once ruled the world from a throne of melted swords, asleep beneath the Cinder Caldera.

The bell is cracking. Each dawn its tone falls a half-step flatter, and in the flat note something deep underground stirs and dreams of waking.

**The hook (level 1):** The party are nobodies in the border village of **Hollowmere** — a hired blade, a hedge-witch's apprentice, a runaway acolyte, a farmer's third son. On the night of the **Falling Star Fair**, a rider dies in the village square with a shard of black bell-bronze clutched in his fist and one word on his lips: *"Cinderwake."* What looks like a bandit problem becomes, by the third clue, the first frayed thread of the end of the world — and the party are the only ones who pulled on it.

---

## Setting & Atmosphere

Vael Anhar is a green, lived-in kingdom of terraced river valleys, bell-towns, and old volcanic ridges gone soft with pine. It is **beautiful and unprepared**. People plant orchards over scorched bedrock and never ask why the deep soil is glass. The air smells of woodsmoke and wet stone; bronze bells answer each other across the hills at dawn like a country humming in its sleep.

Then the omens begin. Frost forms in the shape of wings. Wells taste of sulfur. The sunset holds an extra red minute too long, as if the day is reluctant to end. Far south, the **Cinder Caldera** breathes a thin grey plume that the old maps label *"chimney — harmless."* It is not a chimney. As the campaign climbs, the palette shifts: green valleys to ash-grey marches to the black-glass throat of the volcano, where the sky itself glows like a forge and snow falls upward as cinders.

The throughline sensation: **a kind world running out of time, and a few people refusing to let it go quietly.**

## Themes & Tone

- **Nobodies become legends.** Every tier should make the party visibly more consequential — from "who are you?" to "the realm holds its breath when you ride in."
- **Coalition, not conquest.** Suvarra cannot be beaten by a sword. She is beaten by a kingdom the party stitches back together — knights, miners, river-folk, even old enemies.
- **The cost is real.** A mentor falls. A town may burn. Allies die at the bridge. Hope is the theme; *free* hope is not.
- **Prophecy is a warning, not a script.** The Ashen Verses can be fulfilled in more than one way. What the party *thinks* the prophecy demands is the campaign's central misread.

Tone is bright and earnest with a low drumbeat of dread underneath — Tolkien's warmth, not grimdark. Let players feel heroic. Make them earn it.

## Factions & Powers

**The Cinderwake Cult ("The Ember-Faithful")**
- *Goal:* Wake Suvarra and serve her, believing the old world must burn to be reforged "pure."
- *Methods:* Sabotage the bell, murder bell-wrights, recruit the desperate and the grieving with promises that "the ash remembers everyone equally."
- *Secret:* Their prophet, **Mother Vane**, does not want to rule beside the dragon — she wants to *replace* her, and has been quietly siphoning Suvarra's waking power into herself.
- *Reaction:* Sees the party first as vermin, then as a genuine threat, then as the only worthy sacrifice for the final rite.

**The Crown of Vael Anhar (the fracturing kingdom)**
- *Goal:* Survive — but its leadership is split between denial, profiteering, and panic.
- *Methods:* The aging **Queen-Regent Isaure** wants to evacuate; the war-hawk **Lord Castellan Brannoc** wants to muster; the merchant houses want to flee with their gold.
- *Secret:* The royal line has known about the bell for generations and *suppressed* the truth to keep the peace — a betrayal the party can expose or protect.
- *Reaction:* Useful and infuriating; a faction to be *unified*, which is the entire point of Act II.

**The Order of the Last Bell (knights)**
- *Goal:* Keep the binding intact at any cost; they are the kingdom's only people who know what the bell really does.
- *Methods:* Monastic warrior-bellringers, dwindling, proud, secretive.
- *Secret:* Their order was founded *by* a survivor of Suvarra's reign — and their oldest vault holds the one weapon that hurt her: the **Tuning Lance**, a spear forged to the bell's exact note.
- *Reaction:* Distrustful of outsiders until the party proves devotion; potentially the party's truest allies and their fallen mentor's home.

**The Glass Wardens (subterranean folk)**
- *Goal:* The volcanic-tunnel-dwelling **Drennic** people want to be left alone; they fled Suvarra underground centuries ago and never resurfaced.
- *Methods:* Isolation, ancient maps, and a hatred of the surface that abandoned them.
- *Secret:* They know the only path *into* the Caldera that doesn't cross open battle — and they remember Suvarra's one true weakness.
- *Reaction:* Hostile, then conditional allies if the party returns something stolen from them generations ago.

## Key NPCs

**Ser Halvard Tinn — the mentor who falls.** A retired knight of the Last Bell living quietly in Hollowmere as the village smith. Gruff, patient, secretly grieving a squad he lost in his youth. *Voice:* short sentences, long pauses, calls everyone "lad" or "lass" regardless of age. He trains the party through Act I, gives them the truth they need, and dies at the Act I climax holding a door so they can run. His death is the campaign's first real price and its recurring ghost — his hammer becomes a relic (see Treasure).

**Captain Reyhan Sole — the recurring rival.** A brilliant, vain mercenary captain who keeps appearing one step ahead of the party — first as a competitor, then as a foe in the Crown's pay, then as a reluctant ally when he finally understands the stakes. *Personality:* charming, cuttingly funny, allergic to being second. *Secret:* his sister joined the Cinderwake Cult; he hunts them out of love, not loyalty. *Voice:* needles the party with nicknames; "still alive? disappointing." A redemption arc earned across all three acts.

**Mother Vane — the prophet of ash.** Serene, maternal, genuinely loving toward her flock — which makes her terrifying. She believes she is saving the world by ending it. *Agenda:* steal Suvarra's crown of fire for herself. *Voice:* warm, slow, never raises her voice, refers to catastrophe as "the kindness." The true late-game antagonist behind the dragon.

**Queen-Regent Isaure — the reluctant crown.** Tired, decent, out of her depth, haunted by her ancestors' cover-up. *Agenda:* protect her people and her conscience, in that order, and they conflict. *Voice:* formal, careful, prone to one honest sentence when she thinks no one important is listening. The keystone of the coalition.

**Lord Castellan Brannoc — the war-hawk.** Loud, brave, wrong about half of everything, right about the half that matters. *Secret:* he's bankrolling defenses with embezzled grain stores and knows the kingdom can't actually feed a siege. *Voice:* booms, slaps tables, secretly writes terrible poetry. Will follow the party if they win one battle his way.

**Orla of the Glass Wardens — the underground guide.** A young Drennic exile who remembers the surface as legend. Curious, blunt, fascinated and repelled by sunlight. *Agenda:* prove her people should rejoin the world. *Voice:* literal, asks startling questions, names things by what they do ("the loud-water" = a river). The key that opens the Caldera path.

**The Bellwright, Master Cael — the keeper of the secret.** Last of the artisans who can re-tune the great bell. *Secret:* he caused the original crack, decades ago, through hubris, and has hidden it ever since. *Voice:* nervous, over-explains, flinches at loud noises. His guilt and skill are both essential at the finale.

**Suvarra the Ember-Crowned — the antagonist.** Not a beast. An ancient, articulate tyrant who genuinely believes she is the natural order — that the strong should burn away the weak and rule the ash. She is *patient*, having waited three centuries, and she negotiates. *Voice:* unhurried, almost gentle, speaks of the party's deaths as a foregone administrative detail. "You have done well to reach me. It changes nothing. Kneel, and I will let your village live a season."

## Key Locations

**Hollowmere (village, Tier 1).** The party's home; a bell-town over old glass. *Hook:* the dead rider and the bell-bronze shard. *Danger/Opportunity:* Cinderwake cells recruiting the grieving; the first dungeon is the flooded **Mereroot Crypt** beneath it, where a stolen bell-clapper points the way to the cult.

**Saltcheer (river-port town, Tier 1–2).** Smuggler-friendly trade hub where the cult fences stolen bronze. *Hook:* follow the shard's maker's mark. *Opportunity:* meet Reyhan Sole; recruit river-folk who will later ferry an army.

**The Last Bell Cloister (mountain monastery, Tier 2).** The knights' fortress-temple. *Hook:* Ser Halvard's true home; the Tuning Lance sleeps here. *Danger:* a cult agent inside the order; the party must earn trust to even enter the vault.

**Vael Anhar, the Crown City (capital, Tier 2–3).** Tiered city of bells and gardens around the Choir Spire. *Hook:* convince a fracturing court to act. *Danger/Opportunity:* court intrigue, the royal cover-up, and the central seat of the coalition the party builds.

**The Ash Marches (blighted borderland, Tier 3).** Where Suvarra's waking already kills the land — grey orchards, glass rivers, refugee columns. *Hook:* the front line; the first town that can actually *burn*. *Danger:* cult raiders and the campaign's first taste of dragon-fire from afar.

**The Glassways (underground Drennic realm, Tier 3).** Cathedral-caverns of black volcanic glass beneath the volcano. *Hook:* the only safe road into the Caldera. *Danger:* the Wardens' hostility, lightless mazes, and the heat rising the deeper you go.

**The Cinder Caldera (the dragon's seat, Tier 3 finale).** The volcano's open throat, a black-glass amphitheater under a sky of fire, where Suvarra's buried throne becomes her waking lair. *Hook:* the end of everything. *Opportunity:* the last stand. The **Ashen Bridge** spans the final chasm — the literal "last bridge" of the campaign's promise.

## Act Structure

### Act I — Embers in the Dark (Levels 1–7)
*Goal:* Survive, learn the truth, and lose enough to commit.
- **Beats:** The dead rider → Mereroot Crypt → the shard's trail to Saltcheer → the cult's first scheme exposed → return to Hollowmere to find a cult raid in progress.
- **Climax (Lvl 6–7):** The **Siege of Hollowmere.** Ser Halvard holds the bell-tower door and dies so the party and villagers escape. They carry his hammer and his last truth: *the bell is breaking, and no one believes it.*
- **Decision Point:** Run and warn the kingdom, or stay and try to hold a doomed village? Either way, Act I ends with the party as marked figures carrying impossible news.
- **Milestone:** Reach **level 7** upon escaping Hollowmere with the truth.

### Act II — Gathering the Realm (Levels 8–14)
*Goal:* Build the coalition. No one will believe you. Make them.
- **Beats:** Win the Last Bell knights (recover the Tuning Lance) → break into the Crown City's politics → expose or bury the royal cover-up → broker peace with the Glass Wardens by returning their stolen relic → blunt the cult in the Ash Marches.
- **Recurring Rival:** Reyhan Sole appears as obstacle, then questions his side as his sister's cult turns murderous.
- **Climax (Lvl 13–14):** The **Muster at Vael Anhar** — a great social-and-military set piece where every faction the party courted shows up *or doesn't*, based on Act II choices. Mother Vane strikes here, revealing herself and stealing the first fragment of Suvarra's waking power.
- **Decision Point:** Spend the gathered army to defend the kingdom's heart, or gamble everything on a strike at the Caldera before Suvarra fully wakes?
- **Milestone:** Reach **level 14** when the coalition is forged (the size and strength of it set by how many factions joined).

### Act III — The Sky Turns to Fire (Levels 15–20)
*Goal:* March on the Caldera and end it.
- **Beats:** The descent through the Glassways → the cult's final rite interrupted → Mother Vane's betrayal of Suvarra (and the party can let them fight, or stop one to face the other) → the waking of the dragon → the last march to the Ashen Bridge.
- **Climax (Lvl 19–20):** **The Last Bridge.** The coalition holds the Ashen Bridge against Suvarra's awakening while the party, the Bellwright, and the Tuning Lance attempt the true unbinding — not to kill the dragon with steel, but to *retune the world's bell* and seal her forever, or destroy her outright if they paid the prophecy's full price.
- **Decision Point:** Seal her (saves more lives, risks her return in another age) or unmake her (permanent, but demands a sacrifice the prophecy named). The mentor's ghost, the coalition's strength, and the relics all bear on which ending is even reachable.
- **Milestone:** **Level 20** at the final confrontation; the campaign ends.

## Signature Encounters

**1. The Falling Star Fair (Lvl 1, social→combat).** *Stakes:* a peaceful festival, a dying rider, a first clue. *Twist:* a cult sleeper agent is already in the crowd, watching who picks up the shard. *Tactics:* a chase through fairground stalls; non-lethal options reward clever play.

**2. The Siege of Hollowmere (Lvl 6–7, combat→tragedy).** *Stakes:* the party's home and Ser Halvard's life. *Twist:* the cult doesn't want to raze the town — they want the **bell-clapper** in its tower, and Halvard knows it. *Tactics:* a defense-in-depth fight with villager units the party can position; the door Halvard holds cannot be saved, only honored.

**3. The Court of Bells (Lvl 10–11, social).** *Stakes:* the kingdom's belief and the coalition itself. *Twist:* proving the prophecy in open court means exposing the royal cover-up — and the party must choose whether to humiliate the Queen they need. *Tactics:* a structured debate; evidence gathered across Act II becomes "ammunition"; allies in the gallery can be swayed in real time.

**4. The Drowned Drum (Lvl 13, exploration→combat).** *Stakes:* recover the Wardens' stolen relic from a flooded cult sanctum. *Twist:* draining the chamber wakes a bound elemental the cult was *also* trying to steal. *Tactics:* environmental puzzle (water, glass, heat) under a combat clock; the prize buys an entire faction.

**5. The Last Bridge (Lvl 19–20, epic finale).** *Stakes:* everything. *Twist:* Suvarra doesn't charge — she *offers terms*, and a tempted ally may break ranks; meanwhile Mother Vane's stolen power makes her a second dragon in waiting. *Tactics:* mass-battle abstraction on the bridge (the coalition's strength = rounds the party gets) wrapped around the personal duel and the unbinding ritual. Multiple win conditions; the relics and sacrifices the party banked across 20 levels all pay out here.

## Mysteries & Twists

**The prophecy — the Ashen Verses:** *"When the bell falls flat and the marches grey, / the crown of cinders shall wake to slay; / and only a crown unworn, ungiven, / shall bind the fire or be by it riven."*

The party (and the kingdom) reads "a crown unworn, ungiven" as *find the lost royal crown and don't let Suvarra take it.* **True meaning:** the "crown" is not gold — it is **Suvarra's own crown of fire**, which can only be neutralized by someone who *refuses* to wear it (rejecting the power it offers). Mother Vane's whole plan is built on the *wrong* reading; she wants to wear it. The party's victory hinges on the right one.

**Clue trail:** (1) bell-bronze shards that hum the binding note; (2) Ser Halvard's dying truth about the bell; (3) the Last Bell vault murals showing a *fiery* crown, not a royal one; (4) the Glass Wardens' memory that Suvarra "was beaten by a beggar who would not be queen"; (5) Mother Vane's own sermons, overheard, revealing she means to *take the crown* — confirming what the party must instead *refuse*.

## The Antagonist's Plan (a Clock)

If the party does nothing, advance one tick per major in-world milestone or session of inaction:

1. **The flat note.** The bell's tone slips; weather sours; first omens. (Now.)
2. **The clapper stolen.** Cinderwake takes the bell-clapper; dawn-ringing fails. Hollowmere falls.
3. **The grey spreads.** The Ash Marches blight outward; refugees flood north; the Crown denies it.
4. **The first rite.** Mother Vane channels Suvarra's waking power; cult zealotry erupts into open uprising.
5. **The eyes open.** Suvarra wakes in the Caldera; the sky reddens kingdom-wide; the plume becomes a pillar.
6. **The first flight.** Suvarra burns a border city as a demonstration and "offers" the Crown vassalage.
7. **The coronation of ash.** Mother Vane attempts to seize the crown of fire; whether she succeeds or Suvarra incinerates her, the kingdom burns within a season. **Endgame: the realm is ash.**

## Treasure & Rewards (growing legendary boons)

1. **Tinn's Hammer (Act I).** Ser Halvard's smithing hammer; warms in the presence of the Cinderwake faithful. Grows: gains the ability to mend a broken object once per day (Act I), then to *re-forge* a sundered magic item (Act III). Carries the mentor's ghost — once per campaign, his voice gives one piece of true counsel.

2. **The Tuning Lance (Act II).** A spear forged to the bell's exact note; the one weapon that bites dragon-scale. Grows from "extra damage vs. Suvarra's brood" to, at full attunement, the instrument required for the unbinding ritual.

3. **The Wardens' Glass Heart (Act II).** Returned-relic-turned-gift: a lens of black volcanic glass. Grants sight in magical darkness and, late, reveals lies when held up to a speaker — invaluable in court and at the bridge.

4. **The Coalition Banner (Act II→III).** Not a weapon — a *boon*. Each faction the party unites adds a sigil; in mass battle each sigil grants the coalition extra resilience. A mechanical record of the realm the party rebuilt.

5. **Mantle of the Unworn Crown (Act III).** Awarded to whoever embodies the prophecy's true reading; lets its bearer *refuse* the crown of fire's offer without being consumed — the key to the sealing ending.

6. **Ember-Quenched Ring (capstone).** Forged at the finale from cooled caldera-glass; grants resistance to fire and, once, the power to shield the whole party from a dragon's breath — the last gift of a beaten volcano.

## Hooks for Player Archetypes & Session Zero

- **The Soldier / Fighter:** Ser Halvard's apprentice; inherits his hammer and his grief.
- **The Faithful / Cleric/Paladin:** drawn to the cracked bell as a holy crisis; may have heard the cult's whisper and refused it.
- **The Outsider / Ranger/Druid:** first to read the omens in frost and water; the land speaks to them before the people listen.
- **The Schemer / Rogue/Bard:** has crossed Reyhan Sole before; the rivalry is personal.
- **The Scholar / Wizard/Warlock:** can read the Ashen Verses — and is the one most likely to misread them.

**Session Zero prompts:** What does Hollowmere mean to you? Who would you cross the kingdom to save? What would you refuse, even to win? (Bank that last answer — the finale will ask for it.) Establish each PC's stake in the *home*, so its loss in Act I lands.

## Running It — Pacing & Tips

- **Milestone leveling**, tied to the act milestones above, not XP. It keeps the epic moving and lets you pace the three acts cleanly.
- **Keep the throughline visible.** End most sessions with one fresh omen (use the tables below) so the dragon feels *closer* even in quiet stretches. The bell falling flatter is your campaign's metronome.
- **Make the coalition tangible.** Track allied factions on a visible roster; let players *see* the banner gain sigils. Act III's battle should feel like the sum of Act II's diplomacy.
- **Reuse the rival.** Reyhan Sole should appear ~every 3–4 sessions. Let his arc bend with how the party treats him; a rival who becomes a friend at the bridge is worth ten new NPCs.
- **Pay the cost honestly.** Halvard *must* fall in Act I; later, let at least one coalition choice cost an ally at the bridge. Hope is earned by loss.
- **Let the prophecy be misread for a long time.** Don't correct the party — let them discover the "crown unworn" themselves. The reveal should reframe the whole war.
- **For shorter tables:** compress each act to ~10 sessions by running one signature encounter per location and narrating travel.
