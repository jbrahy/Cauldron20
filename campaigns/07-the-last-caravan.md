# The Last Caravan

> Forty days until the Ash Road closes forever. One caravan left. Cross the Glathmyr — the desert that remembers everyone who has ever crossed it — and pray it has forgotten you.

## At a Glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Level Range** | 3–10 (begins at 3, expect ~9–10 by the gates of Meridian) |
| **Party Size** | 3–5, framed as sworn caravan guards (the *Wardens of the Wheel*) |
| **Est. Length** | 14–20 sessions / one long arc |
| **Tone** | Desert survival, road-trip mystery, escalating moral pressure |

## Premise & The Hook

The Glathmyr was once a sea. The gods drowned a tyrant-god named **Osrek the Patient** beneath it, salted the water with his blood, and the sea died into a poison waste. For three centuries the only survivable crossing has been the **Ash Road** — a chain of warded oases maintained by the **Wheelwright Guild**. Now the wards are failing. The Guild has declared the Road will be sealed in **forty days**, after which the desert reclaims it utterly.

One caravan remains: **the Mercy Train of House Vellic** — eleven wagons, ninety-one souls, refugees from the drought-emptied east racing for **Meridian**, the last green city. The party is hired (or pressed, or fleeing something themselves) as its sworn guards.

**The Hook:** At the muster yard, a hooded Guild factor pays the party double the agreed sum to also protect *one specific wagon* — Wagon Seven, chained shut, smelling faintly of brine and lightning. "You guard the people," she says. "But if it comes to a choice, you guard the cargo. Ask nothing." She is dead by dawn, her tongue cut out. The caravan rolls at first light regardless. The clock has started.

## Setting & Atmosphere

**The Glathmyr** is white. Not sand-gold — *salt-white*, a cracked hardpan that throws sun back into the sky until noon has no shadows. Beneath the crust lies grey poison-silt; break through and you sink into something that burns. The wind tastes of copper and old funerals. At night the temperature plunges and the salt flats *glow* faintly where Osrek's blood seeps closest to the surface.

The desert **remembers**. Travelers who die in the Glathmyr leave impressions — footprints that walk themselves at dusk, a wagon-wreck that re-crashes every night, a song with no singer. The longer you stay, the more the waste seems to know your name. This is not (only) ghosts. It is the buried god, half-dreaming, reading the minds of everyone who treads on his grave.

**The caravan** is its own claustrophobic world: the groan of dry axles, rationed water measured by the thimble, the smell of ninety-one unwashed bodies and frightened oxen, lantern-light and low songs after dark, and the constant political weather of too many desperate people in too small a space. Safety is a circle of wagons. Outside the circle is the desert. Inside it is everyone who might betray you.

## Themes & Tone

- **Scarcity as the real monster.** Water, shade, and trust are all finite and all draining. Most sessions, the scariest number on the table is the water clock, not a monster's hit points.
- **The stranger's debt.** These refugees are nobody to the party — until they have names, faces, and children. The campaign asks repeatedly: who do you carry, and who do you leave?
- **The past doesn't stay buried.** Everyone on this caravan is fleeing something. The desert drags it back up.
- **No clean wins.** Resources spent saving one waypoint are gone at the next. Make the players feel the arithmetic of survival.

Keep the tone tense but not grim-for-its-own-sake. Let there be campfire warmth, gallows humor, and small mercies — they make the hard nights land harder.

## Factions & Powers

**House Vellic (Caravan Masters).**
*Goal:* Get the caravan — and its profit — to Meridian intact. *Methods:* Discipline, hoarded supply, quiet bribery. *Secret:* House Vellic is bankrupt; this crossing is a last gamble, and Matriarch Vellic has secretly promised Wagon Seven's cargo to a buyer in Meridian to clear her debts — she does not fully know what it is. *Reaction to party:* Useful tools to be controlled; warm to those who keep order, cold to anyone who stirs the refugees.

**The Sundered Wheel (Raider Clans).**
*Goal:* Plunder the last caravans before the Road dies; survive the coming famine. *Methods:* Ambush at waterless stretches, poisoning oases, "tolls." *Secret:* They are *also* refugees — exiled Wheelwrights cast out when the Guild rationed who could cross. Their warlord, **Ḥarun One-Eye**, wants passage to Meridian, not gold. *Reaction:* Hostile, but bribable with water or a promise of a place in line.

**The Choir of the Patient Deep (Cult of the Buried God).**
*Goal:* Wake Osrek and let him reclaim the sea/desert, "ending thirst forever." *Methods:* Sabotage the wards, sing the god awake at the deep places, harvest the dying. *Secret:* Wagon Seven's cargo is the keystone of their plan — and they have a sleeper aboard the caravan. *Reaction:* Patient, smiling, never openly hostile until the final act. They believe the party are pilgrims who simply don't know it yet.

**The Wheelwright Guild (Pursuers / Cleaners).**
*Goal:* Recover or destroy Wagon Seven before it reaches Meridian; close the Road on schedule. *Methods:* A mounted retrieval team — the **Lasthands** — under cold Reeve **Calyx Morr**. *Secret:* The Guild *created* the threat in the cargo and wants no witnesses. They will sacrifice the whole caravan. *Reaction:* Will pose as fellow travelers, then as rescuers, then strike.

## Key NPCs

**Tҍ Vellic** — Caravan Matriarch. Iron-spined, exhausted, fair in the way of someone keeping a terrible ledger. *Secret:* Sold the cargo's destination to clear House debt. *Voice:* Speaks in weights and balances; "Everything costs. The trick is choosing the currency."

**Sister Idu** — Refugee field-healer, runs the wagon-bed infirmary. Warm, profane, the caravan's true moral center. *Secret:* Is the Choir's sleeper agent and hates herself for it; joined when her village died of thirst. *Mannerism:* Hums hymns while she works — including, unknowingly, the waking-song.

**Cob Ardent** — Twelve-year-old water-runner, orphan, knows every wagon's gossip. *Agenda:* Wants the party to adopt him. *Secret:* Saw who killed the Guild factor. *Voice:* Breathless, fast, trades secrets for sips of clean water.

**Reeve Calyx Morr** — Guild Lasthand commander, posing as a stranded merchant rescued at the first oasis. Polite, precise, lethal. *Secret:* Has orders to burn Wagon Seven *and everyone who's seen it.* *Mannerism:* Cleans his nails with a salt-pick while delivering threats as pleasantries.

**Old Reshk** — Blind former Wheelwright, the only soul who has crossed the Glathmyr nine times. *Agenda:* Survive one last crossing, then die under green trees. *Secret:* He knows what Wagon Seven holds — he helped seal it twenty years ago. *Voice:* Riddling, smells the wind to navigate; "The salt's lying to us tonight. It does that when *he* dreams."

**Marn & Pell Osswen** — Married wagonwrights, comic relief and the caravan's heart, expecting their first child mid-crossing. *Agenda:* Get the baby to Meridian. *Secret:* Pell is Sundered Wheel by blood and her brother rides with Ḥarun. *Mannerism:* Finish each other's sentences; argue lovingly about everything.

**The Veiled Passenger ("Salt")** — A masked figure in Wagon Eight who pays in pre-poison silver coin and never eats in daylight. *Agenda:* Reach the deep places. *Secret:* Is Osrek's *last priest*, three centuries old, kept alive by the buried god, and the Choir's true hierophant. *Voice:* Whispers; the temperature drops a few degrees when they speak.

**Bantram Vellic** — The Matriarch's soft, frightened nephew and the caravan's quartermaster. *Agenda:* Not die; impress his aunt. *Secret:* Has been skimming the water ration and the deficit is about to become lethal. *Mannerism:* Over-explains, sweats, "It's all accounted for, I assure you, all *perfectly* accounted for."

## Key Locations

**The Muster Yard (Karrick's Gate).** Last walled town before the waste. *Hook:* Hire on, meet the ensemble, watch the factor die. *Opportunity:* Final chance to buy water, salt-cloaks, and oxen — and to overhear who else is desperate to board.

**Thimblewell Oasis.** First waypoint; a ward-stone keeps the water clean. *Hook:* "Rescue" the stranded Calyx Morr here. *Danger:* The ward is already cracking — drink wrong and the Rot takes you. A body in the well no one will claim.

**The Drowned Mast.** A ship's mast juts from the hardpan, all that remains of the old sea. *Hook:* The desert re-enacts a centuries-old shipwreck at dusk; the ghost-crew can be questioned. *Opportunity:* They know a hidden cistern — if the party survives reliving their drowning.

**Saltmother's Maw.** A sinkhole-canyon, Sundered Wheel territory. *Hook:* Toll-ambush, or parley with Ḥarun One-Eye. *Danger/Opportunity:* The only fast route bypasses two oases; taking it means raider escort but a brutal waterless leg.

**The Choir's Hollow.** A salt-cathedral of wind-carved spires where the buried god lies closest. *Hook:* Idu's hymns grow stronger here; the Veiled Passenger slips away to pray. *Danger:* Spend a night and the desert reads every secret aboard — accusations and paranoia detonate.

**Lastward Oasis.** The final functioning ward-station, Guild-held. *Hook:* The Lasthands drop their disguise here. *Danger:* Calyx Morr offers a deal: hand over Wagon Seven and walk to Meridian rich, or be named saboteurs and hunted the last leg.

**The Salt Gate / Meridian's Edge.** Where the white waste breaks against green hills. *Hook:* Climactic convergence — Choir, Guild, and raiders all arrive at once as the Road seals behind. *Opportunity:* Whatever the party decides about the cargo happens *here*, in full view of the city that may or may not open its doors.

## Act Structure

**Act I — The Mercy Train (Days 1–12, Lv 3–4).**
*Goal:* Get rolling, bond with the ensemble, smell the wrongness. *Beats:* The factor's murder; first water crisis; "rescuing" Calyx Morr; first Sundered Wheel skirmish; Cob hints he saw something. *Decision Point:* When water runs short at Thimblewell, do they ration the refugees, the oxen, or the secret cargo's cooling-brine? *Branch:* Their choice sets which NPCs trust them for the rest of the run.

**Act II — The Desert Remembers (Days 13–28, Lv 5–7).**
*Goal:* Survive the deep waste while the mystery tightens. *Beats:* The Drowned Mast; parley or war at Saltmother's Maw; a night in the Choir's Hollow where the desert airs everyone's secrets; Marn & Pell's baby comes at the worst moment; the first sabotage of Wagon Seven. *Decision Point:* A storm forces a route split — fast/raider-escorted vs. slow/safe. *Branch:* Determines whether Ḥarun becomes ally or final foe.

**Act III — The Sealing (Days 29–37, Lv 8–9).**
*Goal:* Reach Lastward as enemies converge and the cargo's nature breaks open. *Beats:* Calyx Morr's ultimatum; the traitor exposed; the Veiled Passenger reveals the Choir's plan; Wagon Seven *opens* (or is forced open). *Decision Point:* Whose plan for the cargo do they serve — Guild, Choir, House, or their own? *Branch:* This is the campaign's spine; commit to whichever the table chooses.

**Act IV — The Salt Gate (Days 38–40, Lv 9–10).**
*Goal:* Cross the threshold as the Road dies behind them. *Beats:* Three-faction convergence; the buried god stirs; the final choice about Osrek's keystone; the gates of Meridian. *Resolution:* Tally who survived, what the cargo became, and whether the desert remembers the party as saviors or just more bones.

## Signature Encounters

**The Thimble War (Social).** *Stakes:* Two factions of refugees come to blows over the day's water ration after Bantram's skimming is discovered. *Twist:* Whoever the party sided with in Act I now expects favoritism. *Tactics:* This is a clock, not a fight — every round of indecision, someone draws a knife. Resolving it without bloodshed earns lasting loyalty.

**Reliving the Wreck (Exploration/Horror).** *Stakes:* To reach the hidden cistern beneath the Drowned Mast, the party must walk the ghost-crew's last night and not "drown" with them. *Twist:* The crew mistakes a party member for someone who betrayed them centuries ago. *Tactics:* Saving throws against suffocation; succeeding by *empathy* (answering the ghosts' grief) rather than force.

**One-Eye's Toll (Combat or Parley).** *Stakes:* Ḥarun's riders box the caravan in Saltmother's Maw. *Twist:* He doesn't want gold — he wants a place in line to Meridian, and Pell Osswen is his sister. *Tactics:* If it goes loud, raiders use the canyon walls and collapse sand-shelves; if it goes to talk, the real fight is convincing Vellic to share the Road.

**The Hollow's Confession (Social/Horror).** *Stakes:* A night in the Choir's Hollow; the buried god whispers each character's hidden secret aloud at the fire. *Twist:* It also exposes an NPC traitor — but only if the party hasn't already earned that NPC's trust. *Tactics:* Pure roleplay pressure-cooker; let player paranoia do the work.

**The Lasthands Strike (Combat).** *Stakes:* Calyx Morr's elite move to seize and burn Wagon Seven at Lastward. *Twist:* Mid-fight, the cargo *responds* to the violence — the keystone pulses, the salt around them begins to liquefy into returning sea. *Tactics:* Disciplined skirmishers with poison-salt blades and a Guild storm-binder; the terrain itself turns hostile as the god wakes.

## Mysteries & Twists

**The Sealed Cargo.** Wagon Seven holds the **Tearstone of Osrek** — a fist-sized crystallized drop of the buried god's blood, the literal *cork* in his grave. While it exists outside the desert's deep places, his dreaming is contained. The Guild dug it up twenty years ago to study (Old Reshk helped seal it again after that went catastrophically wrong) and is now smuggling it to Meridian to weaponize — or to bury where no cult can find it. Returning it to the deep wakes the god. Destroying it floods the Glathmyr back into a poison sea. Carrying it to Meridian hands a god's blood to ambitious men. *No option is clean.*

**The Traitor.** **Sister Idu** is the Choir's sleeper — but a *reluctant* one, broken by grief, hummingthe waking-song without fully meaning to. The true mastermind is the **Veiled Passenger ("Salt")**, Osrek's last priest. The reveal lands best as a betrayal of love: the caravan's kindest soul is the knife.

**Clue Trail:** (1) The factor's cut-out tongue — silenced before she said *what* the cargo was. (2) Cob saw a *hooded singer* near the murder — matches Idu's prayer-shawl. (3) Idu's hymns visibly strengthen the desert's "memory" near deep places. (4) Salt pays in *pre-poison* silver — coin that hasn't existed for 300 years. (5) Old Reshk recognizes the brine-smell of Wagon Seven and goes white. (6) The Tearstone pulses in time with Idu's humming. Layer these so the table assembles it themselves.

## The Antagonist's Plan (a 40-Day Clock)

If the party drifts or fails, the world moves anyway. Advance the clock each waypoint.

- **Days 1–10:** Choir sabotages two oasis wards. Water margin tightens from "tight" to "dangerous."
- **Days 11–20:** Calyx Morr's Lasthands close from behind; raiders harry the flanks. Salt begins night-prayers at every deep place.
- **Days 21–30:** Idu's unwitting hymns wake Osrek's *dreaming*. The desert's "memory" turns hostile — wrecks attack, footprints hunt.
- **Days 31–37:** Salt reaches the Choir's Hollow with the Tearstone (if it falls to them) and begins the final waking-song. The Road begins to seal early as wards collapse.
- **Days 38–40:** **The Sealing.** If the party did nothing: Osrek wakes, the Glathmyr floods back to poison sea, Meridian's gates slam shut against the tide of refugees, and the Mercy Train becomes a salt-line of bones the desert will replay forever. The doomsday is the *default*; every player action buys against it.

## Treasure & Rewards

- **Saltcloak of the Ninth Crossing** (Old Reshk's, if earned): once per day, ignore a desert-heat or thirst penalty and reroll one save against the Rot.
- **The Wheelwright's Compass-Awl:** points not north but toward the nearest clean water within a day's travel; hums when water is poisoned.
- **Pre-Poison Silver (Salt's coin):** worthless as money, but each coin can negate one instance of the buried god's "memory" effect when pressed to the tongue. A non-renewable resource — spend wisely.
- **Ḥarun's Oath-Token** (if allied): a bone whistle that summons 1d4 Sundered Wheel riders once, anywhere in the waste.
- **Brine-Forged Blade:** a weapon quenched in poison-sea; deals extra corrosive damage but slowly rusts gear stored beside it.
- **The Tearstone itself:** a campaign-defining artifact — a god's blood. Whoever holds it at the Salt Gate decides the fate of the Glathmyr. Treat it as plot, not loot.

## Hooks for Player Archetypes & Session Zero

- **The Protector:** the refugees are your charge; the campaign will test what that vow costs.
- **The Mystery-Lover:** the cargo, the murder, and the 300-year-old coin are yours to unravel.
- **The Survivor:** you've crossed wastes before; lean into resource mastery and navigation.
- **The Fugitive:** the desert remembers — perfect for a character outrunning their own past, which *will* surface in the Hollow.
- **The Believer:** faith means something here, with two gods (the drowned and the drowners) in play.

**Session Zero asks:** What are you fleeing east *from*? Who on this caravan would you die for, and who would you abandon? What's your relationship to thirst, to gods, to keeping a hard promise? Build the Wardens of the Wheel as a sworn unit with one shared debt.

## Running It — Pacing & Tips

- **Run travel in legs, not miles.** Each waypoint-to-waypoint leg is one "scene block": a navigation/supply roll, one ensemble beat, one encounter, one night-camp. Don't simulate every dune.
- **Keep two clocks visible.** The **40-Day Road Clock** (doom) and the **Water Clock** (immediate). Players should always be able to see both ticking. Update them out loud.
- **The ensemble is the campaign.** Rotate 2–3 NPCs into focus per session; give each a want, a fear, and a secret. Kill an NPC the party loves *once* mid-campaign to make the stakes real — earned, not arbitrary.
- **Make scarcity tactile.** Track water in physical tokens. When the pile shrinks, the table feels it more than any number.
- **Let the desert speak.** Open each night-camp with one sensory "memory" — a wrong footprint, a voice in the wind, the salt glowing brighter. Foreshadow Osrek constantly; reveal him slowly.
- **Honor the choice in Act III.** Whatever the table decides about the Tearstone, commit hard and play it out. There is no "right" ending — only the one they bought with forty days of hard arithmetic.
