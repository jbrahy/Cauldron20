# Ash & Iron

> The forge-god is dying — and an empire of living fire is learning, too late, that faith does not burn as long as coal.

## Premise & The Hook

For nine centuries the dwarf-hold of **Korrinhal** has lived inside the throat of a volcano named **Vael-Drûm**, the Anvil-That-Wakes — and inside that volcano sleeps **Tuld**, the Forge-Father, a god you can walk up to and touch. Tuld is the fire. Tuld is the heat in the rails, the warmth in the cradles, the glow that keeps the deep-cold from creeping up the stairs.

Three winters ago, Tuld began to cool.

The lava-veins run sluggish and black-skinned. The Ember-Wells gutter. Whole districts have gone dark and frozen, and the refugees come up out of the deeps in their thousands — soot-faced, frostbitten, carrying everything they own. The city that remains, **Hearthgate**, is the last warm ring around the dimming god, and it is full to bursting, rationing heat by the hour, fuel by the ingot, food by the spoon.

The party arrives at Hearthgate as the gates are about to close for the last time.

**Hooks (pick one, or weave several):**
- **The Last Caravan.** The PCs are escorts (paid in *heat-credit*, the only currency that matters now) for a refugee column that must reach Hearthgate before the passes freeze. Halfway up, the column is attacked — and the thing attacking them is made partly of brass.
- **The God's Summons.** A dying Ember-Priest seeks them out by name, claiming Tuld *spoke* — three words, *"Find the splinter"* — and died, sort of, in his arms. He has a map fragment etched on cooling iron.
- **The Recruitment.** A machinist-guild factor offers the party a contract: descend into the dead districts and recover **Foundry Black** (refined fuel) abandoned during the evacuations. Easy money. The deeps disagree.

## Setting & Atmosphere

**Hearthgate** is a city built like a furnace and rationed like a lifeboat. Bronze pipes the width of corridors run hot water and steam-heat through every wall; where they've gone cold, families nail blankets over the openings and burn furniture. The air tastes of sulfur, hot metal, wet wool, and woodsmoke. Bells don't ring the hours — they ring the **heat-shifts**, telling each district when its allotment of warmth flows and when it's cut. People sleep in shifts, in shared "warm-rooms." Children are born and named beside the still-living **Cradle-Vents**, and being *born cold* — in a district without heat — is a class stigma that follows you for life.

**The Deeps** below are what Korrinhal used to be: cathedral-foundries, mile-deep lava-galleries, switchback stairs carved for a population ten times what survives. Now they are going dark from the bottom up. The **deep-cold** is not merely absence of heat; it is hungry, patient, and it *reaches*. Things live down there now that never could before: pale, slow, thriving in the dying god's chill. Frost grows on iron meant to be eternally warm. Whole guild-halls stand frozen mid-evacuation, kettles still on cold hobs, a child's mechanical toy halted between steps.

Land the atmosphere with the senses: the *drop* in temperature when a heat-shift ends mid-scene. The smell of a district that has burned its last chair. The specific, awful quiet of a foundry with no fire in it.

## Themes & Tone

This is a campaign about **what you burn to stay warm** — and that question is literal (fuel, food, faith) and moral (who freezes so others don't?). It asks whether a dying god deserves loyalty or a decent burial, and whether the cold machinery that could replace him is salvation or just a different master. There are no clean answers; the priesthood is sincere and the machinists are right about the danger, and both will get people killed.

Land it by keeping the **clock visible** and the **scarcity real**. Make players spend heat-credit. Make them choose which district gets the recovered fuel. Let them meet Tuld and fail to save him — failure here is not a wipe, it's a *world*. Avoid grimdark nihilism: the warmth between people is the whole point. A shared meal in a cold city should feel like a victory.

## Factions & Powers

**The Ember Conclave (priesthood).**
*Goal:* Reignite Tuld at any cost; restore the old covenant. *Methods:* Sacred rites, fuel-hoarding for the Great Reignition, control of the **Last Forge**. *Secret:* The High Kindler, **Mother Vossa**, has known for a year that the standard rites no longer work — and has been hiding it, staging "minor revivals" with smuggled Foundry Black to keep the faithful from rioting. *Reaction:* Sees the party as either pilgrims or heretics depending on their stance toward the machinists; pays in relics and access.

**The Coldhand Concordat (machinists).**
*Goal:* Replace Tuld with the **Iron Heart**, an artificial geothermal-furnace engine that needs no god. *Methods:* Engineering, salvage, propaganda, and machine-grafting volunteers to survive the cold. *Secret:* The Iron Heart works — but its first prototype already requires a *living* operator fused into it permanently, and they're scaling that up. *Reaction:* Recruits competent outsiders aggressively; despises the Conclave; will lie about the Heart's true cost.

**The Sootborn Assembly (refugee underclass).**
*Goal:* Survival, recognition, a vote in their own fate. *Methods:* Strikes, mutual aid, occasional sabotage; they control the labor the city runs on. *Secret:* Their firebrand leader, **Karsk Ninefingers**, is in secret talks with *both* great factions and will sell to whoever guarantees his people heat — even at a terrible price. *Reaction:* Treats the party as potential champions or scabs; warm to anyone who feeds the cold districts.

**House Velour (opportunists).**
*Goal:* Profit from the collapse; emerge owning whatever's left. *Methods:* Heat-credit lending at ruinous rates, fuel cartels, information brokering. *Secret:* They've been quietly *buying frozen districts* for nothing, betting one faction will win and make the deeds priceless. *Reaction:* Friendly, transactional, treacherous; great patrons, worse friends.

## Key NPCs

- **Mother Vossa, High Kindler.** Ancient, soot-veined dwarf with a voice like banked coals. *Personality:* Maternal, exhausted, iron-willed. *Secret:* The rites are dead and she's hidden it. *Mannerism:* Warms her hands on anything — mugs, lamps, your shoulder — constantly.
- **Engineer-Primus Halt Coldhand.** Machinist leader, half his face replaced with brass and frost-glass. *Personality:* Brilliant, kind, terrifyingly certain. *Secret:* He's the Iron Heart's first living operator-to-be and knows it will erase him. *Mannerism:* Taps a count on his brass jaw when thinking; speaks of Tuld in the past tense.
- **Karsk Ninefingers.** Sootborn firebrand, missing a finger per district he's lost. *Personality:* Charismatic, furious, pragmatic. *Secret:* Negotiating with both sides. *Mannerism:* Counts the missing finger when he makes a threat.
- **Pell, the Ember-Boy.** A child who can still *feel* Tuld's warmth where others can't — a living dowsing-rod for the god's failing pulse. *Personality:* Solemn, too old for his years. *Secret:* Tuld is "talking" to him, and it frightens him. *Mannerism:* Hums the heat-shift bells under his breath.
- **Factor Esmadine Velour.** House Velour's smiling envoy. *Personality:* Warm, generous, lethal. *Secret:* Holds deeds to a third of the dead city. *Mannerism:* Offers you the warmest seat, then prices it.
- **Old Brann, the Stoker.** Keeper of the Last Forge's fires for sixty years. *Personality:* Gruff, grieving, devout. *Secret:* He has fed his own rations into the god for months and is dying of it. *Mannerism:* Names each shovelful of coal.
- **The Cinder-Maiden.** A frost-touched horror, once a priestess, now the deep-cold's herald. *Personality:* Sorrowful, hungry, patient. *Secret:* She's what a *person* becomes when the cold loves them. *Mannerism:* Speaks in old prayers, slightly wrong.

## Key Locations

- **The Last Forge.** Tuld's literal heart-chamber; the god is a slow, dimming mountain of cooling iron-flesh you can stand before. *Hook:* You can meet your god here. *Danger:* Speaking to it costs *years of your own warmth* (exhaustion); failing it on-screen is possible.
- **The Cradle-Vents (Hearthgate's heart).** Where the warm-born live; the city's last reliably-heated ward. *Hook:* Safe-ish base of operations. *Opportunity:* Allies, intel, the refugee camps at its frozen edge.
- **The Iron Heart Works.** The Concordat's grafting-foundry, all brass and frost-glass and the screams of volunteers. *Hook:* The future, if you want it. *Danger:* They want to "improve" you.
- **The Drowned Galleries.** Lower foundries where cooling lava and seeping melt-water made black-ice lakes. *Hook:* Foundry Black caches sunk below. *Danger:* The ice is thin, the water deep-cold-killing, and something swims.
- **Vossa's Hidden Stores.** A sealed reliquary of smuggled fuel the Conclave is hoarding. *Hook:* Enough heat to save a district — or expose the High Kindler's lie. *Danger:* Conclave zealots, and the moral weight of theft.
- **The Stair of Ten Thousand.** The grand evacuation stair, switchbacking from Hearthgate to the dead deeps. *Hook:* The main artery for expeditions. *Danger:* Cold-things hunt the dark turnings; refugees still climb it, freezing as they go.
- **The Cinder-Shrine.** A frost-cathedral where the Cinder-Maiden holds court among the beautifully frozen dead. *Hook:* She knows *why* Tuld is dying. *Danger:* She'll tell you — for a warmth you can't get back.

## Act Structure

**Act I — The Closing Gate (Levels 1–3).** *Goal:* Arrive, survive, earn a place in Hearthgate. *Beats:* Escort/arrival hook; first deep expedition for fuel; witness the heat-rationing and refugee crisis; first brush with cold-things and brass-grafts. *Decision point:* When the party recovers fuel, who gets it — the Conclave's Reignition, a freezing refugee district, or sold to Velour for leverage? *Branch seed:* This choice sets the factions' opinion of the party.

**Act II — Whose Fire (Levels 4–6).** *Goal:* Understand the collapse and pick a lane. *Beats:* Meet Tuld at the Last Forge (and feel him dying); learn the rites are dead (Vossa's lie); tour the Iron Heart Works and its true cost; the Sootborn near open revolt. *Decision point:* **Reignite or Replace.** Commit to relighting Tuld, or to powering the Iron Heart — or to a wild third path (peaceful shutdown / merging god and machine). *Branch:* The chosen faction becomes ally; the spurned one becomes obstacle or enemy.

**Act III — The Splinter (Levels 6–8).** *Goal:* Reach the truth of *why* Tuld dies and act on it. *Beats:* The Cinder-Shrine revelation; the journey to the god's wounded core; confronting the antagonist of the chosen branch (Vossa's desperation, Halt's self-erasure, or the deep-cold's herald). *Decision point:* The final rite/ignition — and who pays for it. *Branch resolution:* Living god restored at cost / cold machine triumphant and human / something new, born of both.

**Optional Act IV — Ashfall (Level 8).** Aftermath and reckoning: govern the saved-or-changed city, face Velour's deeds coming due, and bury or crown what you made.

## Signature Encounters

- **The Brass on the Mountain (combat, Act I).** A refugee column is hit by **Frost-Grafts** — cold-killed dwarves reanimated with salvaged machine-parts by the deep-cold. *Stakes:* Civilians freeze if the party turtles. *Twist:* One graft still pleads in a dead loved-one's voice. *Tactics:* Grafts ignore cold and target heat-sources (lamps, the warm PC). Protect the column's brazier or lose them.
- **The Heat Allotment Hearing (social, Act I/II).** All four factions argue before the Wardens for the next heat-shift's distribution; the party is asked to testify. *Stakes:* A district freezes tonight based on the outcome. *Twist:* Velour quietly bribes whoever speaks well. *Tactics:* No combat — track each faction's favor as a resource the party spends rhetoric on.
- **The Drowned Gallery Dive (exploration, Act II).** Recovering Foundry Black from beneath black-ice. *Stakes:* Each round in the deep-cold water risks exhaustion levels. *Twist:* The cache is guarded by the Cinder-Maiden's frozen "children." *Tactics:* Light = life; the ice cracks under group weight; manage who carries the warmth.
- **Audience With a Dying God (exploration/social, Act II).** Standing before Tuld at the Last Forge. *Stakes:* The god can answer one true question — at the cost of a permanent point of Constitution / a level of lasting fatigue, narratively "your warmth given to the fire." *Twist:* Tuld doesn't ask to be saved. *Tactics:* This is a scene, not a fight; let it be quiet and devastating.
- **The Reignition / The Iron Heart (climax, Act III).** The branch-dependent finale: the Great Reignition rite under siege, or the Iron Heart's first full ignition with a living operator. *Stakes:* The city's survival and a named NPC's life. *Twist:* The chosen method works — but demands a willing sacrifice the party must name. *Tactics:* Waves of cold-things or machinist-zealots while the party "feeds" the ritual/engine over rounds; protect the operator/altar.

## Mysteries & Twists

**The central mystery: why is Tuld really dying?** Not age, not abandonment. Centuries ago Korrinhal mined too deep and *cracked the god's anvil-bone* — a wound called **the Splinter** — and has been quietly bleeding his heat into the deep-cold ever since. The collapse isn't a god giving up; it's a god slowly hemorrhaging from an injury the dwarves caused and the Conclave covered up generations back.

**Trail of clues:** (1) Old Brann's whisper that "the fire runs *out* somewhere, not just down." (2) Conclave records with three centuries deliberately missing. (3) Pell's terror that Tuld "is leaking." (4) The Drowned Galleries getting *colder the deeper you go*, against all physics — the cold is being *fed*. (5) The Cinder-Maiden, who was the priestess sent to seal the Splinter and failed, becoming its herald. (6) The Last Forge's wound, visible only to those who pay the warmth-price to truly see.

**Secondary twists:** Vossa's faked revivals; Halt's planned self-erasure into the Iron Heart; Karsk playing both sides; Velour owning the corpse of the city. The deepest gut-punch: *the Iron Heart runs on the same heat Tuld is leaking* — the machinists aren't replacing the god, they're scavenging his blood.

## The Antagonist's Plan — A Clock

The true antagonist is **the Splinter / the deep-cold** itself, advancing if the party does nothing. Advance one segment per in-world week of inaction (or each time the party fumbles a major faction choice).

1. **The Lower Galleries fall silent.** Last deep districts freeze; refugee surge overwhelms Hearthgate's edge.
2. **The Cinder-Maiden's court grows.** Frost-grafts begin climbing the Stair of Ten Thousand.
3. **Vossa's lie breaks.** A staged revival fails publicly; the Conclave fractures; fuel riots.
4. **The Concordat seizes the Cradle-Vents** to power the Iron Heart, cutting heat to the poor; the Sootborn revolt.
5. **The Last Forge goes cold.** Tuld falls fully dormant; Hearthgate's core heating fails.
6. **Ashfall.** The deep-cold reaches the surface ring; Hearthgate becomes a tomb-city; survivors flee into a freezing world. *Game over for Korrinhal as a living place.*

## Treasure & Rewards

- **Emberheart Coal (boon).** A lump of Tuld's living fire. Bonus action to flare: grants resistance to cold and sheds warm light for an hour; recharges only at a living forge. Three "true sparks" inside it can be spent for greater miracles — and never replaced.
- **The Stoker's Shovel (weapon).** Old Brann's iron shovel; a warhammer-equivalent that deals extra fire damage and can "bank" a hit's heat to warm an ally instead next turn.
- **Frost-Glass Eye (item, machinist-made).** Replaces an eye; grants sight in magical darkness and through cold-fog — but the bearer always feels cold, and creatures of the deep-cold notice them first.
- **Heat-Credit Writ (resource boon).** A House Velour bearer-note for a winter's worth of heat for one district. Spend it to save a district outright — a one-time narrative trump card.
- **Reignition Censer (relic).** Conclave artifact that can rekindle a dead forge or hearth once per long rest; in the finale, channels the Great Reignition.
- **Splinter-Iron (crafting material).** A shard of Tuld's wounded anvil-bone. Forged into a weapon, it deals extra damage to cold-things and hums when the deep-cold is near — but slowly chills its wielder's dreams.

## Hooks for Player Archetypes & Session Zero

- **The Faithful / Cleric / Paladin:** Tuld is a god you can *meet and fail*. Wire their faith to Vossa or to a personal vow to the Forge-Father.
- **The Tinker / Artificer:** The Concordat wants you; the Iron Heart is your dream and your nightmare.
- **The Refugee / Folk Hero / Outlander:** You came up out of the deeps. A frozen district is your old home; Karsk knew your family.
- **The Mercenary / Rogue:** Velour and the heat-credit economy are your playground — until the city's death makes profit hollow.
- **The Scholar / Ranger:** The Splinter mystery is yours to unearth; the deeps' new ecology is unstudied.

**Session Zero:** Agree on tone (this gets cold and sad — confirm the table wants that). Lines & veils for famine, refugee suffering, religious despair, and body-horror grafting. Establish the **X-card / pause** norm. Decide as a group whether you're inclined toward *reignite*, *replace*, or *don't know yet* — and tell the GM you reserve the right to change. Clarify that **failure is a state of the world, not a TPK**: the city can fall and the story continues.

## Running It — Pacing & Tips

- **Make scarcity tactile.** Track heat-credit, fuel ingots, and rations as real numbers the party spends. A short rest in a cold district should cost something. Don't simulate it spreadsheet-deep — three resources, visible to all, is plenty.
- **Keep the doom-clock on the table.** Show the six segments. Advance it on inaction and on botched choices so players feel time as the antagonist.
- **Run refugee morale as a clock too.** A simple 1–6 "Hope" track for the Sootborn: feeding districts raises it, broken promises drop it; at zero, the revolt fires regardless of plot.
- **Let the factions react, not wait.** When the party backs one side, the others *move* — recruit, sabotage, deal. The world should feel like it's contesting the party.
- **Protect the quiet scenes.** The shared meal, the audience with Tuld, a refugee child's name-day — these land the theme harder than any fight. Don't rush them.
- **Allow failure to be meaningful.** If Tuld dies on the party's watch, that's a *legendary* session, not a bad one. Lean in.
