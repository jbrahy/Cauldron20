# Hex of the Pale Court

> The sun set over the Vale of Mournhollow one hundred years ago and never finished rising. Now a grey perpetual dusk hangs like wet linen over the fog, and the Baroness keeps her larder warm. You came in through the only road. There is no longer a road.

## Premise & The Hook

The Vale of Mournhollow is a domain of dread — a pocket of reality folded shut around the sin of one woman. A century ago, **Baroness Ilsavet Cordrane** murdered the only person who ever loved her without wanting something, and in the moment of that murder a dark power answered her grief. It gave her what she begged for — that the night would never end, that nothing she loved would ever leave her again — and made it a cage. The fog at the Vale's edge does not part. Travelers walk into it and walk out the other side back into the Vale, dazed, two hours later, swearing they went straight.

**The Hook (pick one):**
- **The Wrong Road.** A storm, a washed-out bridge, a sign that wasn't there an hour ago — the party takes shelter in Mournhollow and wakes to find the fog has closed. The innkeeper won't meet their eyes.
- **The Summons.** A black-edged invitation, delivered by a raven-masked courier, names each PC by a secret they thought no one knew. The Baroness "requests the pleasure." Declining is not an option once you've read it.
- **The Hireling's Trail.** A patron's missing daughter sent one last letter postmarked from a town that isn't on any map. It ended mid-sentence.

Within the first hour the players must learn the central, terrible rule: **you cannot leave until the curse breaks, and the curse is the Baroness.**

## Setting & Atmosphere

Mournhollow is a long, drowned valley between black pine ridges, lit by a sun stuck three fingers below the horizon — a permanent bruise-coloured almost-dawn. Clocks run slow. Mirrors fog from the inside. The fog itself is a presence: it smells of cold tallow and old roses, it muffles sound so screams arrive late, and it has opinions about who walks home alive.

Lean on the senses of decay-with-manners: the court dresses in mouldering finery and powders the rot with rosewater. Wine is poured from crystal into the cups of the living so their blood will taste of it. The villagers are clean, fed, and quietly numbered — livestock kept gentle. Bells toll the "Vigils" that structure the endless night: Lauds (the court rises), Sext (the Choosing, when names are read), and the dreaded **Matins**, when the Baroness walks. When you describe the Vale, describe captivity disguised as hospitality. Everything is offered. Nothing is free.

## Themes & Tone

- **The land is the prison.** Frustrate escape, but reward cleverness about *why* escape fails. Despair is a tool; do not let it become the only note. Always leave a thread.
- **Complicity.** Survival here means accepting blood-wine, taking the court's favors, looking away at the Choosing. Make the party complicit in small ways so the reckoning lands.
- **The monster is a mirror.** Ilsavet is a warning, not a punchline. Her tragedy is that she got exactly what she asked for.
- **Dark power at a price.** The dark force that made the Vale offers the party gifts. Each one is real, useful, and a small step toward becoming the next Baroness.

## Factions & Powers

**1. The Pale Court (Ilsavet's inner circle).**
*Goal:* Maintain the endless feast and the Baroness's contentment forever. *Methods:* Ritual predation, the Choosing, suffocating "protection." *Secret:* The court is dying of boredom and several members would welcome the curse breaking — if someone else's hands did it. *Reaction:* Gracious, insinuating, possessive.

**2. The Scions of the Wither (rival vampire bloodline).**
*Goal:* Supplant Ilsavet and rule the Vale themselves. *Methods:* Whispered rumor, staged "failures" at court, courting the party as catspaws. *Secret:* They cannot leave either; freedom is a lie they tell to recruit. *Reaction:* Seductive, conspiratorial, treacherous.

**3. The Lantern Oath (doomed resistance).**
*Goal:* Smuggle the living out / failing that, end the Baroness. *Methods:* Sewers, forged Choosing-lists, hoarded fire and silver. *Secret:* Their leader is the Baroness's own great-grandson, mortal, and she knows it and lets him live. *Reaction:* Desperate, brave, easily betrayed because they trust too fast.

**4. The Circle of the Nine Cards (fortune-teller's coven).**
*Goal:* Survive, and steer events toward an outcome only they have foreseen. *Methods:* Card readings that are genuinely prophetic, cryptic aid, debts. *Secret:* They engineered the party's arrival; the "wrong road" was their working. *Reaction:* Riddling, transactional, never quite lying.

## Key NPCs

- **Baroness Ilsavet Cordrane — the Pale Court's mistress.** Ancient grief in a young woman's body; gracious, melancholy, terrifyingly lonely. *Secret/Agenda:* She wants to be stopped but cannot let herself be; her true death requires her own consent. *Voice:* Speaks softly, finishes your sentences, calls everyone "my dear heart." Touches her own throat when she lies.
- **Maître Caul, the Seneschal.** Withered majordomo who runs the court's machinery. *Agenda:* Preserve order at any cost; he fears the void after Ilsavet more than he fears her. *Secret:* He drafted the original cursed pact and has spent a century editing it for loopholes. *Voice:* Dry, precise, counts on his fingers. Never blinks.
- **Lady Sorrowmere (Veska of the Wither).** Beautiful scion, the party's "friend at court." *Agenda:* Use them to kill Ilsavet, then take the throne. *Voice:* Warm, flattering, asks about your dead. Laughs a half-second too late.
- **Tobin Ashling, the Lantern Oath's leader.** Mortal, mid-thirties, hollow-eyed, brave past sense. *Secret:* Ilsavet's living blood-descendant. *Voice:* Talks fast and low, grips your forearm, says "we have until Matins."
- **Mother Nine, of the Card Circle.** Ageless reader who deals the party's fate face-up. *Agenda:* Fulfill a prophecy she will not name. *Voice:* Speaks only in the second person about your future. Smells of beeswax.
- **Pellam the Vintner.** Village wine-maker who presses the blood-wine. *Secret:* He poisons select casks for the Oath, slowly. *Voice:* Cheerful, guilty, over-explains.
- **The Choirless Child (Wynn).** A village girl whose name has been read three Choosings running and who keeps not dying — Ilsavet won't take her. *Secret:* Wynn is the reincarnation of the woman Ilsavet murdered. *Voice:* Solemn, sings a tune Ilsavet wrote a century ago.
- **Old Harrowgate, the Gravekeeper.** Tends a cemetery where no one stays buried. *Agenda:* Remembers the truth everyone forgot. *Voice:* Riddles, then sudden brutal clarity.

## Key Locations

1. **Castle Cordrane** — fog-wreathed eyrie above the Vale. *Hook:* The only place the curse can be broken. *Danger/Opportunity:* The court at full strength; but its mirror-less ballroom hides the Cradle Room where the curse was born.
2. **Mournhollow Village** — clean cobbles, shuttered windows, the Choosing bell. *Hook:* The living, the Oath's hideouts, the inn that won't let you leave. *Danger:* Informers. *Opportunity:* Allies and the truth of daily predation.
3. **The Weeping Fen** — the drowned lower valley, where the fog pools thick. *Hook:* A sunken chapel holds Ilsavet's wedding records. *Danger:* Drowned-dead and will-o'-fog. *Opportunity:* Proof of who she killed.
4. **The Vintry & Cask-Cellars** — Pellam's domain. *Hook:* Blood-wine production. *Opportunity:* Poison the court's supply; smuggle routes. *Danger:* Caul audits the casks.
5. **The Nine-Card Caravan** — the Circle's wagon-ring at the Vale's frayed edge, nearest the fog. *Hook:* Readings, prophecy, the one place the fog "listens." *Opportunity:* Learn the true weakness via the cards.
6. **The Unquiet Cemetery** — Harrowgate's graves that empty themselves. *Hook:* Ilsavet's own tomb-that-waits, built and never used. *Danger:* The risen. *Opportunity:* Her true name, on a stone she had carved for herself.
7. **The Glass Conservatory** — the court's pleasure-garden under a black-glass dome, sunless roses fed on the dead. *Hook:* Court intrigue happens here. *Danger:* Social ruin, a duel. *Opportunity:* Turn scion against seneschal.

## Act Structure

**Act I — The Door Closes (Lv 4–6).** *Goal:* Realize you're trapped; survive your first Choosing; pick a faction to trust. *Beats:* The fog seals; an NPC the party likes is named at the Choosing; first audience with Ilsavet (charming, chilling). *Decision Point:* Whom do you believe — Sorrowmere's escape promise, the Oath's rebellion, or Mother Nine's cards? *Branching:* This sets your primary ally and your first read on the "weakness."

**Act II — The Court's Game (Lv 6–9).** *Goal:* Earn standing at court while secretly assembling the means to break the curse. *Beats:* A masquerade; a scion-vs-seneschal feud the party can tip; the first dark gift is offered. *Decision Point:* Which weakness do you pursue — destroy her relic, kill her bloodline, or win her *consent*? Each path closes others. *Branching:* Taking dark gifts shifts the ending.

**Act III — The Withering (Lv 9–11).** *Goal:* Spring the chosen plan; survive the court's full retaliation. *Beats:* Betrayal by the catspaw faction; the Oath's hideout falls; the truth of Wynn revealed. *Decision Point:* Mercy or destruction for Ilsavet — and what to do with the dark power now offered openly.

**Act IV — Matins (Lv 11–12).** *Goal:* The final confrontation in the Cradle Room. *Beats:* Ilsavet's true death requires her consent, which requires the party to *reach the grieving woman, not fight the monster.* *Branching ending:* free the Vale and walk into a real dawn; or seize the curse and become its next keeper.

## Signature Encounters

1. **The Choosing (social/horror).** *Stakes:* A named villager — maybe one the party loves — must walk to the castle. *Twist:* The party can volunteer a substitute, intercede with a favor owed, or do nothing and live with it. *Tactics note:* No initiative. Track who looked away.
2. **The Masquerade of the Sext Bell (social/intrigue).** *Stakes:* Standing at court; a chance to plant rumor between scion and seneschal. *Twist:* Ilsavet attends unmasked and asks a PC to dance — and to tell her one true thing. *Tactics:* Run as a clock of social "beats"; failure means a duel at dawn-that-never-comes.
3. **The Fen Chapel Dive (exploration).** *Stakes:* Recovering the wedding records. *Twist:* The drowned chapel "remembers" and replays the murder around them. *Tactics:* Hazard + ghost-echo, not a brawl; the danger is despair and drowning fog.
4. **The Oath's Last Stand (combat).** *Stakes:* Tobin's hideout is found. *Twist:* Saving him reveals he's Ilsavet's blood — killing him is a "weakness" path the party may not want. *Tactics:* Caul's hunters use the fog; light and silver matter; running is valid.
5. **Matins in the Cradle Room (finale).** *Stakes:* Everything. *Twist:* Fighting Ilsavet is unwinnable while she refuses death; the win condition is making her *want* to be free — showing her Wynn, naming her victim, offering the mercy she never got. *Tactics:* Alternate combat rounds with "memory" rounds; her HP doesn't matter, her resolve does. The court attacks; the dark power offers the throne mid-fight.

## Mysteries & Twists

- **The curse's origin.** A century ago Ilsavet, low-born and married into the Cordrane name, loved her ward **Mireille** — the one person who wanted nothing from her. Cornered by court scandal and her own jealousy, she killed Mireille and begged the night never to end so she'd never face the morning after. The dark power obliged. *Clue trail:* fen chapel records (the marriage was a sham; Mireille was the real love) → the unused tomb she carved for herself → Wynn singing Mireille's song → Harrowgate's blunt confession.
- **The ruler's true weakness.** Not stake, not sun (there is no sun), not fire. Ilsavet can only be ended by her own consent, granted when confronted with Mireille's death and offered genuine mercy. The "weaknesses" the factions sell — destroy the relic, kill the bloodline — are real enough to *weaken* her but each carries a moral cost the party will regret. *Clue trail:* Mother Nine's cards name "the willing dark"; Caul's edited pact reveals the consent clause he's hidden for a century.
- **The reincarnation.** Wynn *is* Mireille, returned. Ilsavet's refusal to take her name at the Choosing is the one act of love she has left.

## The Antagonist's Plan (a Clock)

If the party drifts, advance the **Withering Clock** (6 segments). Each Vigil cycle or major inaction fills one.

1. The next Choosing claims an NPC ally the party failed to protect.
2. The Lantern Oath is betrayed; a safe route closes.
3. Sorrowmere's scions gain ground; the court splits, hunts intensify.
4. Blood-wine rationing ends; open predation begins in the village.
5. Tobin is taken to the castle as "honored guest" (hostage).
6. **Eternal Lauds:** Ilsavet, content and unchallenged, lets the Vale calcify — survivors become permanent livestock, and the window to break the curse shuts for a generation. (Reset to a far grimmer Act III.)

## Treasure & Rewards

1. **The Sunless Lantern.** Sheds light that the fog cannot drink; reveals illusions and the truly dead. Mundane to recharge but priceless here.
2. **Mireille's Locket.** Holds her portrait and a lock of hair. Useless in a fight — decisive in the finale. The single most important "item" in the Vale.
3. **Casks of Pellam's Vintage.** Poisoned blood-wine; a one-use way to weaken the entire court before the finale at the cost of mass-poisoning (a moral weight).
4. **The Card of the Open Road (dark gift).** Mother Nine's gift: once, ignore the fog and step anywhere in the Vale. *Cost:* each use ages a randomly-chosen villager a year.
5. **The Baroness's Favor (dark gift).** A boon of vampiric vitality — resistances, a charming presence. *Cost:* you stop needing to sleep, then stop wanting to leave. Tracks toward becoming the next keeper.
6. **Letters of the Withered Court** — leverage documents that let the party blackmail one scion into a single betrayal of Ilsavet.

## Hooks for Player Archetypes & Session Zero

- **The Investigator:** the curse is a mystery with a solvable answer; reward digging.
- **The Protector:** the village and the Oath give people worth saving.
- **The Schemer:** four factions to play against each other.
- **The Tempted:** the dark gifts are written for the player who eyes power.
- **The Tragic Heart:** Ilsavet's story rewards empathy over violence.

**Session Zero / horror calibration.** This campaign goes to dark places — predation, control, despair. Establish lines and veils. Confirm the table wants a villain they may pity and an ending they may "lose" by winning wrong. Agree on a pacing valve (the X-card or a check-in word). Decide together: are dark gifts on the table, and does the group want the "become the next Baroness" ending to be reachable? Set the dread dial: *creeping unease* vs. *full gothic horror.*

## Running It — Pacing & Tips

- **A closed domain needs forward motion, not freedom.** Never let escape "work," but always answer *why* it failed with a new clue. Frustration is fine; pointlessness is not.
- **Dread is rhythm.** Alternate the suffocating (Choosings, Matins) with reprieve (a warm inn, an ally's loyalty). Constant horror numbs; contrast cuts.
- **Run a tragic villain as a person.** Let Ilsavet be kind. Let her remember birthdays. The horror is that she means it. Save her monstrousness for the moments it costs the party something.
- **Fortune-card foreshadowing.** Deal a real card to a player at the start of each Act (use the Appendix table). It will come true — let the table see the gears of fate turn. This is your best dread engine and your best clue-delivery.
- **The Vigils as a clock.** Structure sessions around Lauds / Sext / Matins. Players should learn to dread the bell.
- **Make complicity stick.** When they accept blood-wine or skip a Choosing, note it. In Act IV, the court will name those choices aloud.
